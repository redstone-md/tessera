# What Tessera does today

This is the detailed record of every surface and widget, with what was tried on a running shell and what was not. The [README](../README.md) gives the overview.

What exists today, by default on every display:

- A top bar with a clock, drawn as floating rounded pills; **Restart Tessera** and **Quit** are at the bottom of the settings. Tried once: a click on **Restart Tessera** put a new shell with its bars in the place of the running one, and `tessera quit` after it brought the taskbar back. Clicking the clock opens a popup with a calendar to page through by month; it closes when clicked again or when another window takes focus. The calendar shows dates only, without events. Below it the popup lists the notifications waiting in the Windows notification center, newest first: application, age, title and up to two lines of text, each with a button that dismisses it, and **Clear all** for the lot. The list is read from Windows every two seconds through the notification listener, which on the one machine it was tried on was allowed without a prompt for a Tessera that is not packaged; if Windows refuses, the list says so. Reading, dismissing one notification and **Clear all** were tried with an earlier version, which removed every notification at once and, where Windows refused that ("element not found"), one by one; **Clear all** now removes, one by one, only the notifications the list holds, leaving those a rule keeps out of the list and those that arrived since it was read, and that was not tried on Windows. What the list shows, the banners and the history are told in the bullets on notifications below; a notification still has no buttons or images.
- An application launcher, opened from the launcher button of the dock (of the bar in the `Windows` arrangement). It lists the shortcuts of the Start menu folders and the packaged (Store) applications Windows reports as installed, narrows them as you type, and starts the selected one with Enter or a click; the arrow keys move the selection and Escape closes it. Until something is written the applications stand as tiles, an icon over a name, as many in a row as the popup is wide for (six in the 640 pixels of the built-in layouts, eight at the most), and all four arrow keys move the selection; once something is written they are a list, one under another, and `view = "list"` on the widget keeps them so always. A layout file written before has the popup 420 pixels wide, which holds three or four tiles in a row, until its `width` is changed or the arrangement is chosen again in the settings. The tiles were not seen on a running shell. The field it is typed into, which the password of a Wi-Fi network is typed into as well, has a caret that the left and right arrows, Home and End move, by words with Ctrl, and a selection: with Shift held, with Ctrl+A, by dragging the pointer, by two clicks for a word and three for everything; Backspace and Delete take away a letter, a word with Ctrl, or what is selected, and Ctrl+C, Ctrl+X and Ctrl+V copy, cut and paste, of which the first two do nothing where the text is hidden behind dots. A text longer than the field moves to keep the caret in sight. None of this was tried on a running shell; tests cover how the caret steps and what is selected and erased, not the drawing or the pointer. Once two letters are written the search also asks the index of Windows Search for the files and folders whose names have, for every word written, a word that begins with it, and lists up to 30 of them after the applications, those changed last asked for first, each with the folder it lies in; Enter or a click opens one with the program Windows has for it. Only names are asked for, so nothing is found by what a file contains, and only what Windows indexes is found. With the `folders` option of the `launcher` widget, such as `folders = ["~/Documents"]` (`folders = []` for no files), the index is not asked and the files under those folders are searched instead: they are read four levels deep each time the launcher opens, without hidden files and folders, and the first 20000 entries are kept. The same is done with Desktop, Documents, Downloads, Pictures, Music and Videos of the user where Windows Search does not answer; that was not tried. A file or folder has the icon the shell draws for it, files of one kind sharing it. The icons of the rows are looked for two per frame, so that a row may show a blank square for a moment and the letters typed are not held back; how long typing waits was not measured. The index answered a question from a test on one computer, in about 60 ms for one three-letter word; the list it fills was not seen on a running shell. Tried on a running shell: two test files, one of them in a subfolder, were listed for the letters typed, each with its folder; opening one was not tried. The pages of the Windows settings, searches of the web, the documents opened lately and the other answers to what is typed come from the runners of the next item. The list of applications is read each time the launcher opens. An application whose shortcut names no usable icon shows a placeholder.
- Answers to what is typed in the launcher, in the manner of KRunner of KDE Plasma. Runners look at the text on the background executor, each on its own, and the answers they are sure of stand above the applications, the others after the files. The calculator works out `+ - * / ^ %` (also `**`, `×`, `÷`, `mod` and `!`), parentheses, the functions `sqrt cbrt sin cos tan asin acos atan sinh cosh tanh ln log log2 log10 exp abs floor ceil round trunc min max`, the constants `pi`, `e` and `tau`, and numbers in hexadecimal, binary and octal (`0xff + 1`, `255 to hex`) with a parser of its own, so nothing typed is run; angles are in radians, `%` after a number is a percentage and between two numbers the remainder, and a plain number or word is answered only after `=`. Conversions take `5 km to mi`, `100 f in c`, `2 GiB in MB` or `30 мм в дюймы` for length, mass, temperature, capacity, area, speed, size of data and time, with English and Russian names of the units; kB, MB and GB are powers of 1000, KiB, MiB and GiB powers of 1024. Currencies (`100 usd to eur`, `$20 to gbp`) are converted with the daily reference rates of the European Central Bank, fetched from the bank when such a conversion is asked and the rates kept in `%LOCALAPPDATA%\tessera\rates.xml` are older than 12 hours; `currency = false` keeps Tessera from fetching and uses the kept file however old, and a currency the bank publishes no rate for, such as the rouble, is said to be unavailable. Enter, Ctrl+C or the button of the row copies a result. `g:`, `ddg:`, `bing:`, `wiki:`, `gh:`, `yt:`, `maps:` and `ya:` search those sites, the option `web` adds keywords, and a last row offers to search the web for anything with the engine `engine` names (DuckDuckGo unless it names another). Open windows are found by their titles and programs, to switch to or, with the button of the row, to close; `kill <name>` ends the processes of the programs of that name once Enter is pressed a second time. Paths, addresses, `shell:` places, `::{CLSID}`, and programs of the App Paths and of `PATH` with their arguments run as the Run dialog runs them, with `%VARIABLES%` replaced; `>` runs the rest in Windows Terminal, or the command prompt where it is not installed; Shift+Enter runs a command in a terminal and Ctrl+Shift+Enter runs a command or an application as an administrator. Locking, sleep, hibernation, signing out, restarting and shutting down are found by their names and by words such as `reboot` or `logout`, all but locking after a second Enter. 78 pages of the Settings app, and 22 items of the Control Panel and tools of Windows such as Programs and Features, the Registry Editor or Device Manager (as Manage devices and drivers), are found by their names in the language of Tessera, in English and by the names Windows users know them by (`devmgmt`, `regedit`, `ncpa`). `:heart` offers emoji and symbols from a built-in table of 420 with English and Russian names, and choosing one copies it. The documents of the Recent folder are found by their names, and with `recent = true` the latest are listed before anything is typed in the list view (the tiles show the applications alone). What is chosen is remembered with how often and how lately in `launcher-history.toml` in the configuration directory, read and written aside, and ranks higher the next time, applications as well. `runners = [...]` chooses the runners. Written against the Windows API and checked to build for Windows; not run on Windows yet. The calculator, the conversions, the reading of the rates, the splitting of commands, the web keywords, the emoji search and the history are covered by unit tests. Not done: an emoji is copied, not typed into the window that was in front; expressions have no variables and no units; the rates are the euro reference rates only; the pages of the settings are a built-in list and are not read from Windows.
- A compact runner, the popup `runner`: a single field near the top of the display, in the middle of its width, as KRunner stands, which answers with the applications, files and runners of the launcher, shows rows only once something is typed, and before that the documents opened lately. It stands in the new popup place `top`, a sixth of the height of the display below its top and without the veil a centered popup has. The action `runner` opens and closes it; it brings no key of its own, because Alt+Space is the window menu of Windows, and a `[[key]]` with `press = "alt+space"` and `action = "runner"` gives it that one. Written against the Windows API and checked to build for Windows; not run on Windows yet.
- The Run dialog, the popup `run`, which the action `run` opens; the action takes Win+R from Windows and is bound to it by default where the keys of Windows are in use (`windows_keys`, on by default when Tessera is the shell). Its field shows the command run last, selected to be typed over, and runs a program, opens a folder (in the file manager of Tessera where that opens folders), a document or an address as the command runner of the launcher understands it. The commands run before, and for a path being typed the entries of its folder, are offered under the field; the arrow keys go through them, Tab or a click takes one. Ctrl+Shift+Enter or the button As administrator runs it as an administrator, and Browse for a file closes the dialog, shows the file dialog of Windows and opens the dialog again with the file chosen. The history is that of the Run dialog of Explorer, RunMRU, read and written the way Explorer does, so the two share it. What cannot be opened is said under the field with what Windows answered, and the dialog stays open; the Shell shows no message of its own then, so a file of a kind no program is registered for is reported instead of asking for a program. Written against the Windows API and checked to build for Windows; not run on Windows yet; the order of RunMRU and the suggestions are covered by unit tests. Not done: the dialog opens at its surface, not in the corner of the display where the one of Windows stands.
- A Start menu around that launcher, in the same popup: above the list a grid of pinned applications, below it buttons that lock the session, put the computer to sleep, sign out, restart and shut down. The button on a row of the list pins or unpins its application, a right click on a pinned one unpins it, and both rewrite `config.toml`; a click on a pinned one starts it. Every power button except the lock asks for a second click within four seconds. The grid and the buttons are the widgets `apps` and `power`, so a layout can leave them out, reorder them or put them elsewhere; `actions` of `power` can also name `hibernate`, which is not among the buttons otherwise, as hibernation is switched off on many machines, and which was never clicked. Tried on a running shell: pinning, unpinning both ways, starting a pinned application, and the first click on the sleep and shut-down buttons with the question going away by itself. No power button was ever confirmed and the lock button was never clicked, so none of the five actions has been carried out by Tessera yet. The Windows key opens it only when `windows_key = "launcher"` is set in `config.toml`, as described below, and a name too long for two lines is cut.
- The virtual desktops of Windows, as the widget `workspaces`, which no built-in layout places: a numbered button for each desktop, the shown one wider and lit, and a click goes to that desktop. Windows gives programs no interface for this, so the list is read every 300 ms from where Explorer keeps it in the registry, and going to a desktop presses the shortcut for the next or previous desktop (Windows+Ctrl+arrow) as often as it is away. `names = ["web", "code"]` under `[workspaces]` has the buttons say those instead of their numbers, the first for the first desktop, and a button is as wide as its name; the names Windows keeps for its desktops are not read. Tried with three workspaces of Tessera's own, the first and third named: the buttons said `web`, `2` and `chat`, and a click on the second lit it. Not tried: names on the virtual desktops of Windows, and changing them while Tessera runs. There is no making or removing of desktops and no moving of windows between them. Tried on one machine, which has one desktop: the widget showed the single button `1`; after a second desktop was made with the Windows shortcut it showed two buttons with the second lit, a click on the first went to the first desktop and a click on the second back, the bar being shown on both, and the widget showed one button again once the second desktop was removed. More than two desktops are untested.
- Workspaces of Tessera's own instead, when `[workspaces]` in `config.toml` says `own = 3` (2 to 9): the same widget then shows that many buttons, a window belongs to the workspace it was first seen on, and going to another workspace hides the windows of the one left (`ShowWindowAsync` with `SW_HIDE`) and shows those of the one gone to, bringing the window forward that was last in front there. A click with the right button on a button sends the window that was last in front to that workspace. `only` and `except` list programs, by the file name of the executable, whose windows are the only ones that belong to a workspace or are seen on every one. A hidden window that shows itself again joins the workspace shown. Every window is shown again when Tessera quits and when `[workspaces]` changes; the hidden windows are written to `hidden-windows` in the configuration directory before they are hidden, and a Tessera that was terminated shows them when it is next started — until then they stay hidden. Tried on a running shell with two windows of a test program and `only` naming it: going to a second workspace and back, sending a window to a third one and going there, the windows coming back on quitting, coming back on the next start after Tessera was terminated, the dock dropping the buttons of the hidden windows, the windows coming back when `[workspaces]` was taken out of the file while they were hidden, windows of a program named by `except` staying shown, and, with tiling on for the same program, the windows hiding and coming back into their tiles. A window of Character Map, started for the purpose, was hidden, shown again and brought back on quitting the same way. The window of a Store application that Windows hosts in a frame of its own belongs to `ApplicationFrameHost.exe` and is listed by that name, not by the name of the application: Calculator, listed as `CalculatorApp.exe`, stayed shown, and listed as `ApplicationFrameHost.exe` it was hidden, shown again twice and brought back on quitting. `keys = "alt"` (any of `alt`, `ctrl`, `shift` and `win`, joined by `+`) makes those keys with a digit go to the workspace of that number and, with Shift as well, send the window that was last in front there; Windows gives these combinations to no other program while Tessera holds them, and one that another program holds already is reported and left out. The virtual desktops of Windows get no such keys. Tried with `keys = "alt"` and a window of the test program in front: Alt+2 went to the second workspace and hid the window, and Alt+Shift+3 sent it to the third, where a click on the widget showed it again; other combinations of keys and a refused combination were not tried. Not tried: other real applications, and a window that shows itself. A window that is hidden leaves sideways and fades, to the left when a later workspace is gone to and to the right for an earlier one, and towards its workspace when it is sent: what moves is a still picture of the window, taken as it is hidden, since Windows offers no way to move the window of another program smoothly. The windows of the workspace gone to come from the other side: a copy of the picture at half the width and height is kept while a window is hidden, slides to where the window stands, the window is shown under it and the picture fades from over it, which takes a quarter of a second before the window answers. At most 16 MB of such pictures are kept, the oldest going first. A window that was not seen leaving, has no picture left, or was moved or resized while hidden appears where it stands, with the fade Windows gives it. A minimized window and one Windows gives no picture of are hidden without motion. Tried with the window of the test program: it was seen leaving to the left with Alt+2 and to the right with Alt+Shift+3, and came back unchanged; it was seen coming from the left once, a click on the button of the first workspace, and had the keyboard afterwards, with the memory of Tessera as before; several windows at once, maximized ones and windows drawn by a graphics card were not tried, nor how much memory the pictures of large windows take for the quarter of a second they last. Without `per_display` the workspaces are the same on every display. A window of a program running as administrator is not hidden. `per_display = true` gives every display a row of workspaces of its own: the widget of a bar shows and changes the workspaces of its display, the keys those of the display the window in front is on (or else the one the pointer is on), the overview those of the display it opens on, and a window belongs to the display it is seen on, so one that is dragged to another display joins the workspace shown there. Tried with two displays and one window of a test program on each, three workspaces: a click on the second button of one bar hid the window of that display alone and lit the button on that bar alone, a click on the second button of the other bar hid the other window, and going back on the first display showed its window again while the other stayed hidden. Tried with the keys `alt` too: Alt+2 with the window of one display in front hid that window alone and lit the second button on that bar alone; Alt+Shift+3 sent it to the third workspace of its display; the window of the other display, moved over by a program, joined the workspace shown there and was hidden with it at the next click, the bar of the display it came from staying as it was; and the overview opened on that display showed both windows on its third workspace. Not tried with it: a window dragged over by hand, and three displays with windows on each.
- An overview of those workspaces, as the widget `overview`, made for a popup and placed by no built-in layout: a card for each workspace with its name or number, how many windows live there and the icon of each, the card of the shown workspace lit. A click on a card goes to that workspace; a click on an icon goes to the workspace of that window and brings the window forward; an icon dragged onto another card has its window live on that workspace from then on, without going there. It shows the windows as icons, not as pictures, and for the virtual desktops of Windows it only says that Tessera keeps no workspaces of its own, as Windows does not tell which window is on which desktop. Tried with two windows of the test program and three workspaces: the cards showed two windows on the first, a click on another card hid both, and a click on an icon brought both back with that window in front; an icon dragged from the first card to the second left one window on each and hid the one that moved. The test program has no icon, so icons of real programs were not seen in the cards.
- Cards: panels of widgets that lie on the desktop. Each `[[card]]` in `config.toml` names a `corner` (`top-left`, `top-right`, `bottom-left`, `bottom-right` or `center`), the distances `x` and `y` of the panel from the two sides of that corner (24 each), its `width` and `height`, the `displays` it is on and the widgets of its `content`, stacked from the top, built-in ones and those of extensions alike. A card is measured within the part of the display that the surfaces leave to windows and moves when that part changes. It is a window of its own below every other window, is not listed among the windows, and a click on it neither takes the keyboard nor raises it; it fades in when it opens. Tried once on Windows 11 build 28000: a card with the forecast of the weather example and one with the clock, the processor and the memory stood where they were put, a test window opened over the first one stayed above it after a click on the card, the keyboard stayed with the test window, and the `Refresh` button of the forecast answered. Changing `[[card]]` while Tessera ran was tried once: the two cards closed and the one that replaced them opened in its corner. With two cards on each of three displays an optimized build held 160 MB of private memory against 141 MB without them, and at most 174 MB while the cards were replaced. Not tried: `Show desktop` (Win+D), under which Windows may cover the cards until a window is shown again, a display with another scale, and desktop icons under a card, which the card covers. A widget in a card opens no popup. While the surfaces are arranged (`Arrange` in the settings) a card shows an outline over itself and is dragged by it: it follows the pointer within the part of the display left to windows, and when it is let go the nearest corner and the two distances from it are written to `config.toml`. Tried once: a card of the top right corner was dragged down and to the left with the first press on it and `x = 324` and `y = 294` were written. Not tried: dragging on a display with another scale, towards another corner, and a card shown on several displays. The `Desktop` section of the settings adds and removes cards and changes the corner, the displays, the size, the two distances and the widgets of each; the cards are opened anew after every change, without taking the keyboard. Tried once: a card was added there, its width was made two steps longer, both were written to `config.toml` and the card stood on the desktop while the settings stayed open. The other controls of the section were not clicked. With the settings open on that section and two cards on three displays the private memory reached 201 MB for a moment, which is above the 200 MB Tessera aims to stay under, and settled at 177 MB.
- The icons of the desktop, drawn by Tessera (`[desktop]`, see CONFIGURATION.md): a window per display shows the files and folders of the user's desktop folder and of the one all users share, merged as Explorer merges them (an item of the user hides one of the same name of everyone's), and This PC, the folder of the user, Network, the Recycle Bin (full or empty) and Control Panel as "Desktop icon settings" of Windows has them shown (`HideDesktopIcons\NewStartPanel`). Names are the Shell's (`SIGDN_NORMALDISPLAY`), so a `desktop.ini` names a folder in the language of the user and shortcuts have no extension; the folder options of Explorer (hidden extensions, hidden and protected files) are followed, `desktop.ini` is never shown unless protected files are, and shortcuts carry the arrow of Windows. The folders are watched (`ReadDirectoryChangesW`) and read again when they change. The icons stand in a grid of cells of each display: aligned to the grid where they were put (the default), anywhere where they were put (`arrange = "free"`), or column after column from the top left of the primary display in the order of `sort` (`auto`); Sort by puts them in that order once. A new icon takes the first free cell, from where it was dropped or made. Where they stand is kept in `desktop-icons.toml` per arrangement of displays, so a display that goes takes its icons to the primary one and they come back with it. Small, medium and large icons, Ctrl+wheel, and View > Show desktop icons. A click chooses an icon, Ctrl and Shift add to the choice or choose a range, a band drawn from the empty desktop chooses what it touches, the arrows move to the nearest icon that way (Shift adds it), typing chooses the next icon whose name begins so, Enter opens, Alt+Enter shows the properties, F2 renames in place (the extension Explorer hides stays), Delete puts into the Recycle Bin and Shift+Delete deletes for good after the Shell asks, Ctrl+C, Ctrl+X and Ctrl+V share the clipboard of files with Explorer, Ctrl+Z and Ctrl+Y take back and do again through the one undo journal of the process the file manager uses, Ctrl+Shift+N makes a folder and F5 reads the desktop and its icons again. Dragging icons moves them, also onto the desktop of another display while the pointer stays over the desktop, and onto a folder or the Recycle Bin moves them into it (Ctrl copies); once the pointer is over anything else the drag goes on as a drag of their files into any program, started from a task of GPUI with nothing of it borrowed, after which GPUI is given the release of the button that OLE took. What other programs drop on the desktop or on a folder icon is taken by Tessera's own drop target, with Shift, Ctrl and Alt doing as in Explorer and the new icons standing where they were dropped. The menu of an icon is in Tessera's style: Open, Open with (the programs the Shell offers, read when the submenu opens), Cut, Copy, Create shortcut, Rename, Delete, Empty the Recycle Bin for the bin, Properties and Show more options, the menu of the Shell as Windows draws it (`SHCreateDefaultContextMenu`), whose Rename, Delete, Paste and New folder the desktop does itself so they stay in the undo journal. The menu of the desktop has View, Sort by, Refresh, Paste and Paste shortcut, Undo and Redo, New (a folder, a shortcut and the kinds of files Windows offers), Add widgets (a card with the clock, the calendar, what plays, the processor, the memory, the battery, the network or the notifications), Arrange cards, Next wallpaper, Display settings, Personalize, Open in terminal and Show more options. Where Tessera is the shell the windows of the icons are there on every display, also with the icons hidden, so the menu is always this one; beside Explorer with Tessera's icons off there is no such window and Explorer's own desktop and its menu stay. Alt+F4 on the desktop runs the action `power-dialog` and closes nothing. Where Tessera is the shell the window of the icons is a child window of its own `SHELLDLL_DefView`; beside Explorer it is a child of a window of the class `Tessera.Icons` kept below every other window, and Explorer's list of icons is hidden while Tessera draws its own and shown again when it stops, quits, or ends in any other way (by the watchdog of the taskbar, which a mark in `%LOCALAPPDATA%\tessera` tells). `layer = "window"` keeps it a window of its own below the others instead, owned by Tessera's desktop where Tessera is the shell; no window of another process is ever its owner. The window is one pixel shorter than a display where it would fill it, so that Windows does not take the desktop for an application shown full screen, and Tessera's own test for that leaves it out. Cards are owned by the icons of their display, so they stay above them. Written against the Windows API and checked to build for Windows; not run on Windows yet. The grid, the arrangement, the places kept per arrangement of displays, the order, the merging of the two folders, what is hidden, typing to find and the names of a rename are covered by unit tests. Not done: whether a window of GPUI shows what it draws as a child window was not seen (`layer = "window"` is the way out where it does not); the places Explorer kept for its icons are not taken over; overlays of shell extensions (OneDrive, Git) and thumbnails of pictures are not drawn, only icons and the arrow of shortcuts; with auto arrangement icons cannot be dragged into another order; files dragged off the desktop and dropped back onto it are not moved; other programs cannot drop onto the Recycle Bin icon; the items of the Shell cannot be renamed; Shift with the arrows adds icons one by one instead of a range; Undo and Redo do not say what they take back; Share is not in the menu of an icon; Wallpaper settings stand in the menu only once a package brings the action `wallpaper-settings`, and Next wallpaper is there until then; beside Explorer, Show desktop (Win+D) may cover the icons as it may the cards.
- A wallpaper of its own, when `[wallpaper]` in `config.toml` lists `images`: picture files (`.jpg`, `.png` and the kinds below) and directories, whose pictures are taken in the order of their names. Tessera draws them behind the icons of the desktop on every display, each scaled to cover it and cut where it does not fit, or, with `fit = "contain"`, shown whole with black beside it, or, with `fit = "stretch"`, given the shape of the display (more ways below; `fit` is not for the video; tried: `contain` with a square picture on a 2560x1440 display, which showed it 1440 wide in the middle with black at both sides; not tried: `stretch`); the first fades in over the wallpaper of Windows, and with several pictures each stays `interval` seconds (600, at least 5) and fades into the next. The wallpaper of Windows is not changed and shows again when Tessera exits. Windows documents no way to draw there: for each display a plain window, not one of the user interface, is made a child of the desktop window of Explorer between its icons and its wallpaper, which is how Windows 11 24H2 and later lay the desktop out; on an earlier Windows the wallpaper is reported as a problem and not drawn. Such a child is drawn only for a program whose manifest says it is written for Windows 10, which is why Tessera carries a manifest of its own. Tried on Windows 11 build 28000 with three displays and two test pictures: on the one display whose desktop was not covered by other windows, the first picture was seen fading in and then fading into the second, and the wallpaper of Windows was back after Tessera was stopped; the other two displays were covered by windows and not looked at, though the list of windows showed one such child for each of the three displays. Each display keeps the picture once, at its own size: with a 3440x1440 and two 2560x1440 displays an optimized build held 129 to 131 MB while a picture stayed and at most 179 MB while one was read, against 95 MB without pictures. Every two seconds Tessera looks whether the displays are still the same and opens the windows anew if not. Changing `[wallpaper]` while Tessera runs was tried once: with one picture set, a second was added and `interval` set to 6, after which the first faded into the second; the table was then removed, the three children were gone and the wallpaper of Windows showed, with the memory held back at 133 MB from 168 MB. Not tried: desktop icons that are shown (they are hidden on that machine), a display being added or removed, and an earlier Windows. With `video` set to a video file, that plays there instead, again and again and without its sound, scaled to cover each display; `images` are then shown only where the video cannot be played, which is reported as a problem. The media engine of Windows reads the file, so the formats are the ones Windows plays (`.mp4` with H.264 was the one tried), and decodes it once for all displays, with the graphics card where that can; each frame is drawn once by the graphics card, at the size of the video or smaller where no display shows it as large, and the compositor of Windows scales that one picture over every display; nothing of a frame passes through the memory of Tessera. While windows cover every display to nine tenths or more the video is held still and not decoded; this is looked at every two seconds. The video is opened eight seconds after Tessera starts at the earliest, so that the memory starting takes for a moment and the memory opening a video takes do not add up. A video takes memory by its size and by how Windows decodes it, which shows only once it plays: where Tessera then holds more than 200 MB of private memory, while the video opens, when it has come in or two seconds later, the video is left out and that is reported as a problem. Tried with a generated 1920x1080 test video (H.264, 30 frames a second, six seconds) on the same three displays. The video was seen playing and moving on all three: on one directly, on the two that windows covered through thumbnails the compositor gives of the desktop window. It came in over the wallpaper of Windows (screenshots taken in a row show the video ever stronger over it before it is alone), and the wallpaper of Windows was back after Tessera was stopped. With a window of a test program laid under all others over the whole desktop, two looks 0.7 seconds apart showed the same frame, and the video moved again when that window was gone. In six runs the optimized build held 143 to 167 MB (170 to 194 MB of private memory, at most 195 MB at any time since its start) and used about seven hundredths of one processor core. A 3840x2160 video of the same kind, added to the configuration while Tessera ran, played, and Tessera then held 201 to 210 MB (239 to 248 MB private, 316 MB for a moment): since then such a video is left out by the rule above, which was seen once, with 275 MB of private memory for a moment and 166 MB after.   - Tessera creates an invisible window of the taskbar's class above Explorer's, which is where applications deliver their icons, and passes every message on to Explorer, so Explorer's own notification area stays complete. At startup it broadcasts `TaskbarCreated`, the message Explorer sends after a restart, so that running applications add their icons again; an application that ignores it shows up only once it changes its icon.

Icons coming and going around the limit were not tried. There are no balloon notifications and no reordering of icons, and an application running as administrator does not receive clicks from a Tessera that does not.

Motion is driven by springs: surfaces slide in from their edge at startup, dock buttons, notification icons and notifications grow in and shrink away as they come and go, the foreground indicator stretches, the wavy lines of the meters travel as their level changes, the output of a `command` fades in when it changes, popups, menus and window previews fade and slide in and out, tiled windows travel to their tiles and back, and buttons respond to hover and press. Nothing else is animated yet: text changes and the clock switch instantly, as does extension content other than its progress bars, and surfaces do not animate out on exit.

While Tessera runs:

- Both surfaces stay above other windows and reserve their screen edge, so maximized windows stop at them. While the window in front fills the whole display, as a game or a video shown full screen does, the surfaces of that display go below all windows, and come back when another window is in front; they look for that every 0.3 seconds. Tried with a borderless test window the size of one display: the bars of that display went behind it and came back when it closed, those of the other displays stayed. Not tried with a game. The window that fills the display also counts while it is not in front, as when the pointer went to another display, if it is the topmost application window of its display; this was reported for a full-screen application on a second display and changed without being tried on a running shell. A surface that sees the frame of its display change, as after another resolution was chosen, reserves its edge anew or goes to its place again; it looks for that every 0.3 seconds too. On a bar where a group at an end reaches into the middle, the middle group stands off the middle only as far as that group needs; where the three are together wider than the bar, the groups at the ends are cut where they do not fit, instead of running over the edge of the display. Widgets have no narrower form to take there. Both were written for reports from a display of 1920 by 1080 pixels and one whose resolution was changed, and neither was tried on a running shell. Windows releases that space when Tessera exits.
- The Windows taskbar is hidden. To stop it reserving space, Tessera switches it to auto-hide, which is a persistent Explorer setting. A second `tessera` process waits for the shell to exit — normally, by crashing or by being terminated — and then restores the previous auto-hide setting and shows the taskbar again. If that watchdog process is itself terminated first, the taskbar stays hidden until you turn off "Automatically hide the taskbar" in Windows Settings or restart Explorer.

Tessera injects nothing into other processes. To learn when windows open, close or come to the foreground it asks Windows for out-of-context window events (`SetWinEventHook`), which arrive in its own process; the list is also read again every five seconds for anything that goes unreported. Anything the taskbar provided that is not listed above has no replacement yet. The Windows key opens the Windows Start menu unless `config.toml` names a popup for it with `windows_key`. Then Tessera watches the keyboard with a low-level hook (`SetWindowsHookEx`), which also runs in its own process: every key is let through, and when the Windows key is released without another key having gone down, a key code that no keyboard has is pressed first, which keeps Windows from opening its menu, and the popup is opened or closed at the widget that opens it with a click. This was tried in a debug build with a window of Tessera or of a test program in front: the launcher opened with its search field ready, the Windows menu stayed closed, and a second press closed the launcher. Two presses 120 ms apart also left the Windows menu closed and the test window in front. Adding the setting to `config.toml` while Tessera ran was tried once as well: the next press opened the launcher. One combination was tried, in a release build with a test window in front: the Windows key with the up arrow maximized that window and the popup stayed closed, and the key by itself then opened and closed the popup. The setting was then taken out of the file while Tessera ran, and the key opened the Start menu of Windows again. Other combinations were not tried.

Measured on one machine (three displays, a release build, a top bar and a dock on each display with the widgets of the `void` layout, the machine otherwise in use): the shell process held about 150 MB of private memory, about 105 MB of it in the working set, and used between 0.35 and 1 second of processor time for every 10 seconds in which it was not touched, nearly all of it on the main thread; the bars were redrawn three or four times a second then. The same six bars with only a clock in each, which were not redrawn once in that time, used about 0.3 seconds for every 10, and a single bar about 0.1: GPUI wakes every window at every refresh of the display whether or not anything changed. Those figures were taken before Tessera held the wakings back: it now lets them through at every refresh only while something of its own moves or its windows get the pointer or the keys, and about twenty times a second otherwise, by putting a function of its own in the place of `RedrawWindow` in the import table of its own program. With that, six bars and six cards (twelve windows) left untouched used between 0.17 and 0.27 seconds of the main thread for every 10 in two runs, where they had used 0.39 before. A wallpaper of Tessera (`[wallpaper]`) keeps the pixels of its picture, one copy for each display, in a temporary file that Windows maps into memory and deletes when Tessera lets go of it: in one run with it the private memory was 156 MB and the working set 137 MB, about 35 MB more than without a wallpaper, which is about the size of the three pictures; that those are the pages of the file, which Windows can take back, was not measured. Opening a popup used to add about 54 MB to the private memory for two to three seconds, which took it above the 200 MB Tessera aims to stay under. GPUI has Windows choose the size a window is made with, about 2580 by 984 pixels on that machine, and what draws the window held pictures of that size (one region of 42 MB and about 11 MB more) until the window had its real size and they were let go. Tessera now has its windows made 64 pixels wide and high, by a hook on the making of windows in its own thread, and a popup, menu, tip or preview that closes is kept hidden, at most two of them, and shown again for the next one. With that, in three runs with six bars and six cards, the private memory was 155 to 159 MB at rest and 162 to 168 MB with a popup open, the first one as the later ones, and no reading in between was higher. What is left is the start: every window costs about 5 MB more for about three seconds after it is made, which was not traced further than to memory that is not of Tessera's own allocator. With six bars and no card the private memory was 133 MB at rest, 142 to 153 MB with a popup open and 173 MB at the most, in one run; with six cards more, made together with the bars, it reached 215 to 231 MB in the first seconds of six runs, above the 200 MB, and was at 155 to 159 MB three seconds later. The cards are therefore opened 3.5 seconds after the bars when the shell starts: with that it was 173 to 179 MB at the most in five runs, the highest of them with a popup opened in the second after the start, and 153 to 156 MB at rest. A menu and a preview of a window, each drawn in a window kept from a popup closed before, took it to 165 to 169 MB in one run. All of these numbers are of GPUI as published. GPUI gives every window an atlas of its own for glyphs and pictures, and its textures are 1024 by 1024 pixels at the least, 1 MB for the glyphs and 4 MB for the pictures, however little the window draws. Tessera is now built with a copy of GPUI 0.2.2 kept in `vendor/gpui`, in which these textures are 256 by 256 pixels at the least and nothing else is changed; a texture that fills up is followed by another one, as before. In one run with six bars, the memory that is there at the most and gone at rest, about 55 MB, was in three heaps of the process, private memory that is no file and no picture; in another run, with the allocator of Tessera counted, what the program itself held was never above 7 MB in the first ten seconds. So these heaps are of what Tessera calls, the system or the driver of the display adapter; which of them was not traced. Of the 155 MB at rest, what Tessera and GPUI hold through the allocator of the program was 6 MB in one run that counted it; the rest is what the graphics and text libraries of Windows and the driver hold for twelve windows, 57 MB of it before the first window is made; two bars with a clock each took 72 to 78 MB at rest and 96 MB at the most. The working set stayed at or below 121 MB in all of these. A flat bar that keeps its text readable reads the desktop under it. Windows charges the process that reads with memory for the whole desktop window, so another process of Tessera is started for each reading, which gives the pixels and ends: one such reading of a strip of 2560 by 36 pixels took 67 ms. What that process is charged with was not measured, and neither was the shell during a reading since; the 212 MB seen once during a reading before may have been the windows of the start instead, which was not told apart then. The bars used to read every five seconds, each on its own; they now have it read one at a time, when the wallpaper is seen to change and once a minute otherwise. After a wallpaper of Tessera changed from dark to bright, the fields under the widgets were there in the next picture taken, 0.9 s later; that was watched once, on one display. The motion of popups was timed once: while the calendar and the settings opened and closed, six animations of 66 to 97 frames, the view was rendered every 6 ms at the median, within 8 ms for 95 of 100 frames and after 13 ms at the longest. The bars were timed the same way: while the pointer went from widget to widget of a bar, four animations of 50 to 92 frames, a bar was rendered every 6 ms at the median, within 8 ms for 95 of 100 frames and after 18 ms at the longest; and while the widgets were arranged by hand and one was dragged along its bar and back, 802 frames in 4.9 seconds, every 6 ms at the median, within 7.4 ms for 95 of 100 and after 16 ms at the longest, three of them later than 12 ms. All of that counts renderings, not frames that reached the display. The dock was timed later, in one run: while the pointer went over its buttons, four animations of 39 to 77 frames, it was rendered every 6 ms at the median, within 8 ms for 95 of 100 frames and after 8.2 ms at the longest; the animation in which the preview of a window opened, 103 frames, had one gap of 85 ms, which is the making of the window of the preview, and was within 7.3 ms for 95 of 100 otherwise. In the same run the Bluetooth popup and the launcher opened and closed in four animations of 65 to 85 frames, every 6 ms at the median and after 8.3 ms at the longest.
