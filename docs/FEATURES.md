# What Tessera does today

This is the detailed record of every surface and widget, with what was tried on a running shell and what was not. The [README](../README.md) gives the overview.

What exists today, by default on every display:

- A top bar with a clock, drawn as floating rounded pills; **Restart Tessera** and **Quit** are at the bottom of the settings. Tried once: a click on **Restart Tessera** put a new shell with its bars in the place of the running one, and `tessera quit` after it brought the taskbar back. Clicking the clock opens a popup with a calendar to page through by month; it closes when clicked again or when another window takes focus. The calendar shows dates only, without events. Below it the popup lists the notifications waiting in the Windows notification center, newest first: application, age, title and up to two lines of text, each with a button that dismisses it, and **Clear all** for the lot. The list is read from Windows every two seconds through the notification listener, which on the one machine it was tried on was allowed without a prompt for a Tessera that is not packaged; if Windows refuses, the list says so. Reading, dismissing one notification and **Clear all** were tried with an earlier version, which removed every notification at once and, where Windows refused that ("element not found"), one by one; **Clear all** now removes, one by one, only the notifications the list holds, leaving those a rule keeps out of the list and those that arrived since it was read, and that was not tried on Windows. What the list shows, the banners and the history are told in the bullets on notifications below; a notification still has no buttons or images.
- An application launcher, opened from the launcher button of the dock (of the bar in the `Windows` arrangement). It lists the shortcuts of the Start menu folders and the packaged (Store) applications Windows reports as installed, narrows them as you type, and starts the selected one with Enter or a click; the arrow keys move the selection and Escape closes it. Until something is written the applications stand as tiles, an icon over a name, as many in a row as the popup is wide for (six in the 640 pixels of the built-in layouts, eight at the most), and all four arrow keys move the selection; once something is written they are a list, one under another, and `view = "list"` on the widget keeps them so always. A layout file written before has the popup 420 pixels wide, which holds three or four tiles in a row, until its `width` is changed or the arrangement is chosen again in the settings. The tiles were not seen on a running shell. The field it is typed into, which the password of a Wi-Fi network is typed into as well, has a caret that the left and right arrows, Home and End move, by words with Ctrl, and a selection: with Shift held, with Ctrl+A, by dragging the pointer, by two clicks for a word and three for everything; Backspace and Delete take away a letter, a word with Ctrl, or what is selected, and Ctrl+C, Ctrl+X and Ctrl+V copy, cut and paste, of which the first two do nothing where the text is hidden behind dots. A text longer than the field moves to keep the caret in sight. None of this was tried on a running shell; tests cover how the caret steps and what is selected and erased, not the drawing or the pointer. Once two letters are written the search also asks the index of Windows Search for the files and folders whose names have, for every word written, a word that begins with it, and lists up to 30 of them after the applications, those changed last asked for first, each with the folder it lies in; Enter or a click opens one with the program Windows has for it. Only names are asked for, so nothing is found by what a file contains, and only what Windows indexes is found. With the `folders` option of the `launcher` widget, such as `folders = ["~/Documents"]` (`folders = []` for no files), the index is not asked and the files under those folders are searched instead: they are read four levels deep each time the launcher opens, without hidden files and folders, and the first 20000 entries are kept. The same is done with Desktop, Documents, Downloads, Pictures, Music and Videos of the user where Windows Search does not answer; that was not tried. A file or folder has the icon the shell draws for it, files of one kind sharing it. The icons of the rows are looked for two per frame, so that a row may show a blank square for a moment and the letters typed are not held back; how long typing waits was not measured. The index answered a question from a test on one computer, in about 60 ms for one three-letter word; the list it fills was not seen on a running shell. Tried on a running shell: two test files, one of them in a subfolder, were listed for the letters typed, each with its folder; opening one was not tried. The pages of the Windows settings, searches of the web, the documents opened lately and the other answers to what is typed come from the runners of the next item. The list of applications is read each time the launcher opens. An application whose shortcut names no usable icon shows a placeholder.
- Answers to what is typed in the launcher, in the manner of KRunner of KDE Plasma. Runners look at the text on the background executor, each on its own, and the answers they are sure of stand above the applications, the others after the files. The calculator works out `+ - * / ^ %` (also `**`, `×`, `÷`, `mod` and `!`), parentheses, the functions `sqrt cbrt sin cos tan asin acos atan sinh cosh tanh ln log log2 log10 exp abs floor ceil round trunc min max`, the constants `pi`, `e` and `tau`, and numbers in hexadecimal, binary and octal (`0xff + 1`, `255 to hex`) with a parser of its own, so nothing typed is run; angles are in radians, `%` after a number is a percentage and between two numbers the remainder, and a plain number or word is answered only after `=`. Conversions take `5 km to mi`, `100 f in c`, `2 GiB in MB` or `30 мм в дюймы` for length, mass, temperature, capacity, area, speed, size of data and time, with English and Russian names of the units; kB, MB and GB are powers of 1000, KiB, MiB and GiB powers of 1024. Currencies (`100 usd to eur`, `$20 to gbp`) are converted with the daily reference rates of the European Central Bank, fetched from the bank when such a conversion is asked and the rates kept in `%LOCALAPPDATA%\tessera\rates.xml` are older than 12 hours; `currency = false` keeps Tessera from fetching and uses the kept file however old, and a currency the bank publishes no rate for, such as the rouble, is said to be unavailable. Enter, Ctrl+C or the button of the row copies a result. `g:`, `ddg:`, `bing:`, `wiki:`, `gh:`, `yt:`, `maps:` and `ya:` search those sites, the option `web` adds keywords, and a last row offers to search the web for anything with the engine `engine` names (DuckDuckGo unless it names another). Open windows are found by their titles and programs, to switch to or, with the button of the row, to close; `kill <name>` ends the processes of the programs of that name once Enter is pressed a second time. Paths, addresses, `shell:` places, `::{CLSID}`, and programs of the App Paths and of `PATH` with their arguments run as the Run dialog runs them, with `%VARIABLES%` replaced; `>` runs the rest in Windows Terminal, or the command prompt where it is not installed; Shift+Enter runs a command in a terminal and Ctrl+Shift+Enter runs a command or an application as an administrator. Locking, sleep, hibernation, signing out, restarting and shutting down are found by their names and by words such as `reboot` or `logout`, all but locking after a second Enter. 78 pages of the Settings app, and 22 items of the Control Panel and tools of Windows such as Programs and Features, the Registry Editor or Device Manager (as Manage devices and drivers), are found by their names in the language of Tessera, in English and by the names Windows users know them by (`devmgmt`, `regedit`, `ncpa`). `:heart` offers emoji and symbols from a built-in table of 420 with English and Russian names, and choosing one copies it. The documents of the Recent folder are found by their names, and with `recent = true` the latest are listed before anything is typed in the list view (the tiles show the applications alone). What is chosen is remembered with how often and how lately in `launcher-history.toml` in the configuration directory, read and written aside, and ranks higher the next time, applications as well. `runners = [...]` chooses the runners. Written against the Windows API and checked to build for Windows; not run on Windows yet. The calculator, the conversions, the reading of the rates, the splitting of commands, the web keywords, the emoji search and the history are covered by unit tests. Not done: an emoji is copied, not typed into the window that was in front; expressions have no variables and no units; the rates are the euro reference rates only; the pages of the settings are a built-in list and are not read from Windows.
- A compact runner, the popup `runner`: a single field near the top of the display, in the middle of its width, as KRunner stands, which answers with the applications, files and runners of the launcher, shows rows only once something is typed, and before that the documents opened lately. It stands in the new popup place `top`, a sixth of the height of the display below its top and without the veil a centered popup has. The action `runner` opens and closes it; it brings no key of its own, because Alt+Space is the window menu of Windows, and a `[[key]]` with `press = "alt+space"` and `action = "runner"` gives it that one. Written against the Windows API and checked to build for Windows; not run on Windows yet.
- The Run dialog, the popup `run`, which the action `run` opens; the action takes Win+R from Windows and is bound to it by default where the keys of Windows are in use (`windows_keys`, on by default when Tessera is the shell). Its field shows the command run last, selected to be typed over, and runs a program, opens a folder (in the file manager of Tessera where that opens folders), a document or an address as the command runner of the launcher understands it. The commands run before, and for a path being typed the entries of its folder, are offered under the field; the arrow keys go through them, Tab or a click takes one. Ctrl+Shift+Enter or the button As administrator runs it as an administrator, and Browse for a file closes the dialog, shows the file dialog of Windows and opens the dialog again with the file chosen. The history is that of the Run dialog of Explorer, RunMRU, read and written the way Explorer does, so the two share it. What cannot be opened is said under the field with what Windows answered, and the dialog stays open; the Shell shows no message of its own then, so a file of a kind no program is registered for is reported instead of asking for a program. Written against the Windows API and checked to build for Windows; not run on Windows yet; the order of RunMRU and the suggestions are covered by unit tests. Not done: the dialog opens at its surface, not in the corner of the display where the one of Windows stands.
- A Start menu around that launcher, in the same popup: above the list a grid of pinned applications, below it buttons that lock the session, put the computer to sleep, sign out, restart and shut down. The button on a row of the list pins or unpins its application, a right click on a pinned one unpins it, and both rewrite `config.toml`; a click on a pinned one starts it. Every power button except the lock asks for a second click within four seconds. The grid and the buttons are the widgets `apps` and `power`, so a layout can leave them out, reorder them or put them elsewhere; `actions` of `power` can also name `hibernate`, which is not among the buttons otherwise, as hibernation is switched off on many machines, and which was never clicked. Tried on a running shell: pinning, unpinning both ways, starting a pinned application, and the first click on the sleep and shut-down buttons with the question going away by itself. No power button was ever confirmed and the lock button was never clicked, so none of the five actions has been carried out by Tessera yet. The Windows key opens it only when `windows_key = "launcher"` is set in `config.toml`, as described below, and a name too long for two lines is cut.
- The virtual desktops of Windows, as the widget `workspaces`, which no built-in layout places: a numbered button for each desktop, the shown one wider and lit, and a click goes to that desktop. Windows gives programs no interface for this, so the list is read every 300 ms from where Explorer keeps it in the registry, and going to a desktop presses the shortcut for the next or previous desktop (Windows+Ctrl+arrow) as often as it is away. `names = ["web", "code"]` under `[workspaces]` has the buttons say those instead of their numbers, the first for the first desktop, and a button is as wide as its name; where it names none, the button says the name the desktop was given in the task view of Windows, read from where Explorer keeps it. Tried with three workspaces of Tessera's own, the first and third named: the buttons said `web`, `2` and `chat`, and a click on the second lit it. Not tried: names on the virtual desktops of Windows, and changing them while Tessera runs. Desktops are made and removed only through the shortcuts of Explorer, and the windows of other programs are not moved between them (see the workspaces made and removed below). Tried on one machine, which has one desktop: the widget showed the single button `1`; after a second desktop was made with the Windows shortcut it showed two buttons with the second lit, a click on the first went to the first desktop and a click on the second back, the bar being shown on both, and the widget showed one button again once the second desktop was removed. More than two desktops are untested.
- Workspaces of Tessera's own instead, when `[workspaces]` in `config.toml` says `own = 3` (1 to 16; that many at first, see below): the same widget then shows that many buttons, a window belongs to the workspace it was first seen on, and going to another workspace hides the windows of the one left (`ShowWindowAsync` with `SW_HIDE`) and shows those of the one gone to, bringing the window forward that was last in front there. A click with the right button on a button opens its menu, whose **Send window here** sends the window that was last in front to that workspace. `only` and `except` list programs, by the file name of the executable, whose windows are the only ones that belong to a workspace or are seen on every one. A hidden window that shows itself again joins the workspace shown. Every window is shown again when Tessera quits and when `[workspaces]` changes; the hidden windows are written to `hidden-windows` in the configuration directory before they are hidden, and a Tessera that was terminated shows them when it is next started — until then they stay hidden. Tried on a running shell with two windows of a test program and `only` naming it: going to a second workspace and back, sending a window to a third one and going there, the windows coming back on quitting, coming back on the next start after Tessera was terminated, the dock dropping the buttons of the hidden windows, the windows coming back when `[workspaces]` was taken out of the file while they were hidden, windows of a program named by `except` staying shown, and, with tiling on for the same program, the windows hiding and coming back into their tiles. A window of Character Map, started for the purpose, was hidden, shown again and brought back on quitting the same way. The window of a Store application that Windows hosts in a frame of its own belongs to `ApplicationFrameHost.exe` and is listed by that name, not by the name of the application: Calculator, listed as `CalculatorApp.exe`, stayed shown, and listed as `ApplicationFrameHost.exe` it was hidden, shown again twice and brought back on quitting. `keys = "alt"` (any of `alt`, `ctrl`, `shift` and `win`, joined by `+`) makes those keys with a digit go to the workspace of that number and, with Shift as well, send the window that was last in front there; Windows gives these combinations to no other program while Tessera holds them, and one that another program holds already is reported and left out. The virtual desktops of Windows get no such keys. Tried with `keys = "alt"` and a window of the test program in front: Alt+2 went to the second workspace and hid the window, and Alt+Shift+3 sent it to the third, where a click on the widget showed it again; other combinations of keys and a refused combination were not tried. Not tried: other real applications, and a window that shows itself. A window that is hidden leaves sideways and fades, to the left when a later workspace is gone to and to the right for an earlier one, and towards its workspace when it is sent: what moves is a still picture of the window, taken as it is hidden, since Windows offers no way to move the window of another program smoothly. The windows of the workspace gone to come from the other side: a copy of the picture at half the width and height is kept while a window is hidden, slides to where the window stands, the window is shown under it and the picture fades from over it, which takes a quarter of a second before the window answers. At most 16 MB of such pictures are kept, the oldest going first. A window that was not seen leaving, has no picture left, or was moved or resized while hidden appears where it stands, with the fade Windows gives it. A minimized window and one Windows gives no picture of are hidden without motion. Tried with the window of the test program: it was seen leaving to the left with Alt+2 and to the right with Alt+Shift+3, and came back unchanged; it was seen coming from the left once, a click on the button of the first workspace, and had the keyboard afterwards, with the memory of Tessera as before; several windows at once, maximized ones and windows drawn by a graphics card were not tried, nor how much memory the pictures of large windows take for the quarter of a second they last. Without `per_display` the workspaces are the same on every display. A window of a program running as administrator is not hidden. `per_display = true` gives every display a row of workspaces of its own: the widget of a bar shows and changes the workspaces of its display, the keys those of the display the window in front is on (or else the one the pointer is on), the overview those of the display it opens on, and a window belongs to the display it is seen on, so one that is dragged to another display joins the workspace shown there. Tried with two displays and one window of a test program on each, three workspaces: a click on the second button of one bar hid the window of that display alone and lit the button on that bar alone, a click on the second button of the other bar hid the other window, and going back on the first display showed its window again while the other stayed hidden. Tried with the keys `alt` too: Alt+2 with the window of one display in front hid that window alone and lit the second button on that bar alone; Alt+Shift+3 sent it to the third workspace of its display; the window of the other display, moved over by a program, joined the workspace shown there and was hidden with it at the next click, the bar of the display it came from staying as it was; and the overview opened on that display showed both windows on its third workspace. Not tried with it: a window dragged over by hand, and three displays with windows on each.
- An overview of those workspaces, as the widget `overview`, made for a popup and placed by no built-in layout: a card for each workspace with its name or number, how many windows live there and the icon of each, the card of the shown workspace lit. A click on a card goes to that workspace; a click on an icon goes to the workspace of that window and brings the window forward; an icon dragged onto another card has its window live on that workspace from then on, without going there. It shows the windows as icons, not as pictures (see the live pictures below), and for the virtual desktops of Windows it only says that Tessera keeps no workspaces of its own, as Windows does not tell which window is on which desktop. Tried with two windows of the test program and three workspaces: the cards showed two windows on the first, a click on another card hid both, and a click on an icon brought both back with that window in front; an icon dragged from the first card to the second left one window on each and hid the one that moved. The test program has no icon, so icons of real programs were not seen in the cards.
  - Live pictures in the overview: each card spreads the windows of its workspace over a room of its own, as Present Windows of KWin spreads them (rows chosen for the largest pictures, each keeping the shape of its window, never enlarged, in the order they stand on the screen), the windows of the workspace shown as live pictures that Windows draws and keeps up to date (`DwmRegisterThumbnail`), the others as their icons, since a window hidden on another workspace, or minimized, has no picture to give. Windows draws a picture over everything the popup draws, so one that the edge of the popup or a scrolled list would cut is not drawn at all. Click and drag work as before. Written against the Windows API and checked to build for Windows; not run on Windows yet. The windows of other workspaces show the still picture taken as they were hidden, where there is one. Not done: the pictures stay fully opaque while the popup fades out.
- Workspaces made and removed while Tessera runs, in a grid, with icons, sticky windows, and the windows going back to their workspaces after a restart. `own` under `[workspaces]` is how many there are at first (1 to 16); `min` (1) and `max` (16) bound how many a row may have. A workspace is made at the end of its row by the action `workspace` with `add` (Win+Ctrl+D where the keys of Windows are bound), which goes there, or by the button `+` the widget ends with under `add_button = true`; one is removed by `workspace remove` (Win+Ctrl+F4, the one shown) or **Remove** in the menu a click with the right button opens on its button. The windows of a removed workspace go to the one before it, or after it for the first, and are shown where that is the workspace shown; the list of hidden windows is written before any window is shown or hidden, and names the windows being shown until they are, so that a Tessera terminated in the middle still shows them all at its next start. Every workspace has a number that stays while it exists, whatever is made or removed before it, written in `workspaces-state.toml`, for the parts that keep something for each workspace; `[[wallpaper.workspace]]` keeps following the place of a workspace, and so do `names` and `icons`, which lose the entry of a removed workspace where all displays share one row. **Rename** in the menu (or `workspace rename`) opens a field with the name in it; Enter writes it into `names` of `[workspaces]` in `config.toml`, keeping the rest of the file as written, and an empty name gives the number back. **Icon** offers twelve characters of the icon font, or none, written into `icons`; an icon is drawn beside the name of a workspace, or instead of its number. **Send window here** sends the window that was last in front there. The wheel over the widget goes to the next or previous workspace, round the ends with `wrap = true`. `rows = 2` lays the workspaces out in two rows, filled from the left: Win+Ctrl+Left and Right (`workspace previous` and `next`) go through all of them in order, `workspace left` and `right` stay in the row, and Win+Ctrl+Up and Down (`up`, `down`) go to the row above or below in the same column, round the ends with `wrap`; the widget shows the rows where a bar is high enough for buttons of 10 pixels or more, and one row otherwise. On a bar higher than wide the widget stands in a column, its menus opening beside the bar at the pointer. `send-window` sends the window that was last in front to the workspace in a direction or of a number without going there (Win+Ctrl+Shift+Left and Right), and `sticky` has the window in front seen on every workspace, or lives on the workspace shown again. The keys of these actions are taken from Windows and bound only where the keys of Windows are in use (by default as the shell); beside Explorer, Win+Ctrl and the arrows stay with Explorer unless `windows_keys = true`. Which window lives on which workspace, the workspace each row shows and the sticky windows are written to `workspaces-state.toml` two seconds after a change and when Tessera quits, each window named by its handle together with its program and when its process started, so that another window that gets the same handle is not taken for it. Quitting still shows every window; at the next start the windows that are still there go back to their workspaces, and those of workspaces not shown are hidden again (a state written for another `own` or `per_display` is not taken up). The overview, the task view and the window switcher can ask for a still picture of a window hidden on another workspace: the half-size picture the passage keeps of it, made into an image once and kept until the window is shown or gone, at most 64 MB of them; a minimized window and one hidden without the motion have none and show their icon. Beside Explorer without `own`, the names given to the virtual desktops of Windows in its task view are read from the registry; which window is on which desktop is asked of the documented `IVirtualDesktopManager` on a thread of its own, every half second while something wants to know and, once something asked, again when a window changes and every two seconds, so that the views listing the windows of every workspace can list those of every desktop of Windows; the first time they ask after Tessera starts the lists are empty until that first answer, about half a second later. The overview, the window switcher, the task view and the dock do not list them yet, and the window menu and the rules offer no desktop of Windows to move a window to. A window of another desktop that is chosen is brought forward and Windows goes to its desktop. Making and removing a desktop of Windows presses Win+Ctrl+D and Win+Ctrl+F4 for Explorer, which works only while Explorer runs, and removes only the desktop shown. Written against the Windows API and checked to build for Windows; not run on Windows yet, and the grid, the removal, the state file and the lists have unit tests, which were checked to build but have not been run (they run only on Windows). Not done: renaming a virtual desktop of Windows, since there is no documented way and a name written into the registry is not known to reach a running Explorer, so Tessera does not write it; moving a window of another program to another desktop of Windows, since `IVirtualDesktopManager::MoveWindowToDesktop` moves only the windows of the program that calls it; showing a window on every desktop of Windows; dragging a button of the widget to reorder workspaces; the window menu and the rules do not offer sticky windows yet; and the settings offer `own` only from 2 to 9 and none of `min`, `max`, `rows`, `wrap`, `add_button` or `icons`, which are set in `config.toml`. Where every display has its own row, a row of a display is made, with its own numbers, the first time the display is seen.
- The menu of an application of the Start menu, as the Start menu of Windows, Kickoff and the applications menu of Seelen have one (this replaces "a right click on a pinned one unpins it" of the bullet above). A right click on a row or a tile of the launcher, the Menu key or Shift+F10 on the selected one, or a right click on a pinned application of the `apps` widget opens it: Open, Run as administrator, Open file location (the shortcut chosen in its folder, in the file manager of Tessera where that opens folders, else in Explorer), Pin to Start or Unpin from Start (where an `apps` widget shows the pins), Pin to the dock or Unpin from the dock (the `pinned` option of the `windows` widgets, written with the name the Start menu shows; Unpin also takes away a pin written as the path of the shortcut), Add to desktop (a copy of the shortcut file in the Desktop folder, or a new shortcut for a packaged application or a program, named so that none is written over), App settings (the page of a packaged application in the Settings app, `ms-settings:appsfeatures-app?<family>`), Uninstall and Properties; a file found by the search has Open, Open file location and Properties, and Run as administrator where it is a program. The menu is drawn in front of the launcher inside its popup, since a window of its own would take the keyboard and close the popup; the arrows, Enter and Escape choose in it. Uninstall asks for a second Enter or click, then removes a packaged application for the user with the package manager of Windows (`RemovePackageAsync`) and says in a notice whether it is gone; a program is removed by the uninstaller it registered under the `Uninstall` keys of the registry of the user and of the machine (both views), found by the folder that holds what its shortcut opens, by its icon, or by its name, and started as the registry writes it, so that it asks for an administrator and tells of itself as it does under Windows; where neither is found, the installed applications of the Settings app open. Written against the Windows API and checked to build for Windows; not run on Windows yet. Which deeds the menu offers, the finding of a program among the Uninstall keys, the splitting of its command line and the pins of the dock are covered by unit tests. Not done: a program found by name alone may be the wrong one where two have alike names; the system applications that Windows does not let a user remove are reported as not uninstalled, with what the package manager answered; the menu has no jump list of the application.
- What the launcher shows before anything is typed, as the option `view` of the `launcher` widget says, in the manner of the default view of the applications menu of Seelen and the tabs of Kickoff: `all` (the tiles of every application, as before; `list` keeps them one under another), `favorites` (the applications pinned to Start, in their order, as tiles) or `categories` (a rail of the categories beside the list of the applications of the chosen one; the left and right arrows go through the categories, and a click chooses one). A category is the first folder below `Start Menu\Programs` of the user or of the machine that a shortcut lies in, two folders of one name being one category; the shortcuts that lie in no folder stand together as Not in a folder, and the packaged applications as Packaged apps, as Windows gives no category for them (an AppInfo has none). `tabs = true` shows a row of tabs above the list that switches the three views; it is there by itself where `view` names favorites or categories. The widget `recommended` shows what the Start menu of Windows 11 recommends: the applications whose shortcuts in the Start menu were made in the last `days` (7), then the files of the Recent folder, at most `shown` (6), `columns` (2) in a row; `files = false` and `apps = false` leave either out. A click opens an entry, and its menu has Remove from list, which deletes the shortcut of a file in the Recent folder, as Windows does, and remembers an application in `recommended.toml` in the configuration directory. Written against the Windows API and checked to build for Windows; not run on Windows yet. The categories, the favorites, the choosing of the view and of the new applications are covered by unit tests. Not done: a packaged application installed lately is not recommended, as it has no shortcut file to date it by; the time a file was opened is not shown, only its folder; the views do not remember the one chosen last.
- More runners for the launcher and the runner (the Run dialog runs commands only), in the manner of the plugins of KRunner. `bookmarks`: the bookmarks of every profile of Edge, Chrome, Brave, Vivaldi and Chromium (their `Bookmarks` files) and of Firefox (the latest of its daily backups, `bookmarkbackups\*.jsonlz4`, unpacked with LZ4), found by a part of the title or of the address and opened with the browser Windows opens addresses with; they are read again after a minute. `spell`: `spell necessary` or a word with a question mark after it (`necesary?`) is checked by the spell checker of Windows (`ISpellCheckerFactory`) in each language of the list of languages of the user that has a dictionary, and offers the words it may be meant as, each copied when chosen. `time`: `time`, `time in UTC+3`, `time tokyo` and conversions such as `15:00 berlin in new york` or `3pm utc to local`, the day changed where it does. `terminal`: the profiles of Windows Terminal (of the Store, of its preview and of a release installed otherwise), opened with `wt -p <name>`, the hidden ones left out. `sessions`: the folders and workspaces Visual Studio Code, its Insiders release and VSCodium keep in their `storage.json` (the recent ones of the File menu, the windows open when it was closed and those with unsaved work), opened in that editor again with `--folder-uri` or `--file-uri`. `unicode`: `U+2192` gives the character of that code, `char right arrow` those whose names (from unicode_names2) have a word starting with each word typed, and `char →` the code and name of a character; choosing one copies it. `help`: `?` lists the runners of the widget with how each is asked, and choosing one puts its name into the field. The name or a short name of a runner and a colon before the text ask that runner alone, as `calc: 2+2`, `kill: notepad`, `char: arrow` or `wt: ubuntu`, and the name with nothing after it tells how the runner is asked; a keyword of a web search keeps its meaning, so a keyword of the option `web` that is also the name of a runner searches the web. The runner names are `bookmarks`, `spell`, `time`, `terminal`, `sessions`, `unicode`, `help` and `extensions`, the last for the runners of extensions, which come with the extension API. The settings of Windows Terminal and of the editors are read with a reader of JSON of Tessera's own that takes their comments and trailing commas. Written against the Windows API and checked to build for Windows; not run on Windows yet. The reading of the bookmarks, the profiles and the editor state, the questions of the time, of spelling, of characters and of the help, and the single-runner mode are covered by unit tests. The `time` runner names zones as the clocks of other time zones do (an IANA identifier, the name Windows gives a zone, or a city), and also by a city Windows lists beside a zone, as `time osaka`. Not done: a bookmark opens in the default browser, not in the browser it was found in; the spell checker is asked for one word, not a sentence.
- Files found by what they hold and by their properties, as Baloo of KDE Plasma finds them, through the index of Windows Search (this replaces "Only names are asked for, so nothing is found by what a file contains" of the launcher bullet above). Every value that goes into the question of the index is written in its quotes with its quotes doubled, or is a number, a day or a kind Tessera writes itself, so what is typed cannot change the question; a filter whose value cannot be read is searched as words. Not done: only what Windows indexes is found, and what files hold only for the kinds it reads; where the index cannot be asked and the folders are read instead, the filters are not applied and only names are searched.

- The icons of the desktop, drawn by Tessera (`[desktop]`, see CONFIGURATION.md): a window per display shows the files and folders of the user's desktop folder and of the one all users share, merged as Explorer merges them (an item of the user hides one of the same name of everyone's), and This PC, the folder of the user, Network, the Recycle Bin (full or empty) and Control Panel as "Desktop icon settings" of Windows has them shown (`HideDesktopIcons\NewStartPanel`). Names are the Shell's (`SIGDN_NORMALDISPLAY`), so a `desktop.ini` names a folder in the language of the user and shortcuts have no extension; the folder options of Explorer (hidden extensions, hidden and protected files) are followed, `desktop.ini` is never shown unless protected files are, and shortcuts carry the arrow of Windows. The folders are watched (`ReadDirectoryChangesW`) and read again when they change; a folder that goes or cannot be reached any more is read once more and no longer watched. Desktop icon settings and the folder options are looked at every two seconds (the registry only) and the desktop read again when they changed. The icons stand in a grid of cells of each display: aligned to the grid where they were put (the default), anywhere where they were put (`arrange = "free"`), or column after column from the top left of the primary display in the order of `sort` (`auto`); Sort by puts them in that order once. A new icon takes the first free cell, from where it was dropped or made. Where they stand is kept in `desktop-icons.toml` per arrangement of displays, so a display that goes takes its icons to the primary one and they come back with it. An arrangement never seen before (a display unplugged for the first time, another resolution) starts from the places of the one most alike, each display following the one of the same frame or else of the same number, so only the icons of a display that went move. Only a move of the user changes the places kept: an icon pushed aside while a display has less room (a larger bar, another scale) goes back once there is room, and a desktop folder that cannot be read for a while (a desktop on a network share that is away) loses no places; the places of icons gone are let go once every folder was read. A display whose window cannot be made part of the desktop gets none, and its icons go to the others. Small, medium and large icons, Ctrl+wheel, and View > Show desktop icons; with the icons hidden the desktop takes no keys but F5, Ctrl+Z and Ctrl+Y, draws no band, takes no drops and fetches no icons. A click chooses an icon, Ctrl and Shift add to the choice or choose a range, a band drawn from the empty desktop chooses what it touches, the arrows move to the nearest icon that way (Shift adds it), typing chooses the next icon whose name begins so, Enter opens, Alt+Enter shows the properties, F2 renames in place (the extension Explorer hides stays), Delete puts into the Recycle Bin and Shift+Delete deletes for good after the Shell asks, Ctrl+C, Ctrl+X and Ctrl+V share the clipboard of files with Explorer (read away from the user interface), Ctrl+Z and Ctrl+Y take back and do again through the one undo journal of the process the file manager uses, Ctrl+Shift+N makes a folder and F5 reads the desktop and its icons again. Dragging icons moves them, also onto the desktop of another display while the pointer stays over the desktop, and onto a folder or the Recycle Bin moves them into it (Ctrl copies); once the pointer is over anything else the drag goes on as a drag of their files into any program, started from a task of GPUI with nothing of it borrowed, after which GPUI is given the release of the button that OLE took. What other programs drop on the desktop or on a folder icon is taken by Tessera's own drop target, with Shift, Ctrl and Alt doing as in Explorer and the new icons standing where they were dropped. The menu of an icon is in Tessera's style: Open, Open with (the programs the Shell offers, read when the submenu opens), Cut, Copy, Share (the share window of Windows), Create shortcut, Rename, Delete, Empty the Recycle Bin for the bin, Properties and Show more options, the menu of the Shell as Windows draws it (`SHCreateDefaultContextMenu`), also for This PC, the Recycle Bin and the other items of the Shell, whose Rename, Delete, Paste and New folder the desktop does itself so they stay in the undo journal. The menu of the desktop has View, Sort by, Refresh, Paste and Paste shortcut, Undo and Redo labelled with what they take back or do again, as in the file manager, New (a folder, a shortcut and the kinds of files Windows offers), Add widgets (a card with the clock, the calendar, what plays, the processor, the memory, the battery, the network or the notifications), Arrange cards, Next wallpaper, Display settings, Personalize, Open in terminal and Show more options. Where Tessera is the shell the windows of the icons are there on every display, also with the icons hidden, so the menu is always this one; beside Explorer with Tessera's icons off there is no such window and Explorer's own desktop and its menu stay. Alt+F4 on the desktop runs the action `power-dialog` and closes nothing. Where Tessera is the shell the window of the icons is a child window of its own `SHELLDLL_DefView`; beside Explorer it is a child of a window of the class `Tessera.Icons` kept below every other window, and Explorer's list of icons is hidden while Tessera draws its own and shown again when it stops, quits, or ends in any other way (by the watchdog of the taskbar, which a mark in `%LOCALAPPDATA%\tessera` tells, or, where Tessera's taskbar is off, when Tessera starts again); Explorer's list is hidden and shown without waiting for Explorer. `layer = "window"` keeps it a window of its own below the others instead, owned by Tessera's desktop where Tessera is the shell; no window of another process is ever its owner. The window is one pixel shorter than a display where it would fill it, so that Windows does not take the desktop for an application shown full screen, and Tessera's own test for that leaves it out. Cards are owned by the icons of their display, so they stay above them. Written against the Windows API and checked to build for Windows; not run on Windows yet. The grid, the arrangement, the places kept per arrangement of displays, the order, the merging of the two folders, what is hidden, typing to find and the names of a rename are covered by unit tests. Not done: whether a window of GPUI shows what it draws as a child window was not seen (`layer = "window"` is the way out where it does not); the places Explorer kept for its icons are not taken over; overlays of shell extensions (OneDrive, Git) and thumbnails of pictures are not drawn, only icons and the arrow of shortcuts; with auto arrangement icons cannot be dragged into another order; files dragged off the desktop and dropped back onto it are not moved; other programs cannot drop onto the Recycle Bin icon; the items of the Shell cannot be renamed; Shift with the arrows adds icons one by one instead of a range; Wallpaper settings stand in the menu only once a package brings the action `wallpaper-settings`, and Next wallpaper is there until then; beside Explorer, Show desktop (Win+D) may cover the icons as it may the cards.

- A notice of a newer release, as the widget `update`, which no built-in layout places: 20 seconds after the start and then once a day it reads the list of releases of the repository from github.com, and once a release is newer than the running version it shows its name; a click opens the page of that release in the browser.   - Without the taskbar of Explorer, as when Tessera is the shell, Tessera shows the desktop itself: a click minimizes every application window that is shown (its own windows stay) and remembers them, and the next click brings back those that are still minimized, the one that was in front last on top; once one of them was brought back by hand, the next click shows the desktop again. The action `desktop` does the same from a `[[key]]` (Win+D, which it takes from Windows) or `tessera do desktop`. Written against the Windows API and checked to build for Windows; not run on Windows yet. Windows may refuse to minimize the windows of an elevated program.
  - The action `desktop` also takes an `arg`: `minimize` minimizes every application window that is shown, as Win+M does, `restore` brings back those it minimized, as Win+Shift+M does, and `others` minimizes all but the window in front, as Win+Home does; these use Tessera's own record even while Explorer runs. Showing the desktop hands the keyboard to the window of the desktop where the shell has one, and otherwise minimizes the window in front so that Windows activates another, so typing does not go to a window that is not seen; a window that does not minimize, such as one of an elevated program, no longer keeps the others from coming back. The windows come back on a thread of their own in the order they were in, with the one that was in front active. Written against the Windows API and checked to build for Windows; not run on Windows yet. Where the keys of Windows are in use, Win+D, Win+M, Win+Shift+M and Win+Home are bound to them (see the keys of Windows below). Not done: a window that does not answer is brought back without its place in the order.
- Keys an action brings by default: a feature can give its action the keys Windows has for the same thing, which are bound after the `[[key]]` entries of the user and only where none of them presses the same keys, when `windows_keys = true` or, without the key, when Tessera is the shell. Checked to build for Windows and tested in unit tests; not run on Windows yet. The actions that bring keys, and the keys they bring, are listed below (the keys of Windows, Win+X, Win+P, the keyboard layouts, and snapping and the window menu, which bring theirs through `snap`, `snap-display`, `snap-layouts` and `window-menu`).
- The power plan of Windows, as the widget `plan`, which no built-in layout places: the name Windows gives the plan in use, read every five seconds while a plan widget is shown. A click opens the popup `plan`, which the module brings along with the widget `plan-list`: every plan Windows lists, the one in use lit, and a click on another puts it in use; the row moves at once and the plans are read again after Windows has answered, away from the thread that draws. `cycle = true` on the widget brings back the old click, which puts the next plan in use, the first after the last (give it `popup = ""` too, or the popup opens as well). Tried once on a running shell, before the list: the widget showed the name of the plan in use. The list and the click were written against the Windows API and checked to build for Windows; not run on Windows yet, since putting a plan in use changes a setting of Windows.
- High dynamic range, as the widget `hdr`, which no built-in layout places: the letters HDR on the bar of a display that is capable of it, lit while the display shows it, asked every five seconds; a click switches it for that display, the way the settings of Windows do. A display that is not capable of it shows nothing. Tried on one machine with HDR off: the widget stood on the bars of two displays, unlit, and answered the pointer. One click on it there switched the display: the pictures taken of the displays four seconds later were black, so the lit widget was not seen; a second click switched it back and the widget stood unlit as before. There is no widget for the night light: Windows documents no way to read or switch it.
- The Recycle Bin, as the widget `trash`, which no built-in layout places: its icon and the number of items in it, counted every three seconds, the icon alone while it is empty; a click has Explorer open the bin. Tried once: the widget showed the number Windows gave for the bin at that moment. Not tried: the click, the number changing, an empty bin.
  - A right click opens a menu: **Open**, and while the bin holds anything **Restore all items**, which asks first in a box of Windows how many items and bytes it moves and, on yes, brings every item back to where it was deleted from (the Shell asks where a name there is taken), and **Empty the Recycle Bin** with the size it frees, which asks Windows to empty the bins of all drives with its own confirmation, progress and sound (an empty bin is left alone, without an error). The widget counts again once that is done. The same is the action `trash`, with `open` (or no `arg`), `restore` or `empty`. Open goes to Explorer, as Tessera's file manager has no view of the bin yet. Written against the Windows API and checked to build for Windows; not run on Windows yet.
- The network connection, in the top bar: an icon for a cable, Wi-Fi or a mobile network with its signal strength, and the name of the network, with "no internet" added when only the local network is reached and "Offline" when nothing is connected. Windows reports changes of the connection; the signal strength is read again every thirty seconds. Seen on a running shell only with a cable connected: Wi-Fi, mobile networks, losing the connection and the report of a change were not tried, and networks cannot be listed or joined from Tessera.
- The Wi-Fi networks in reach, in a popup that a click on the network connection opens: each with its signal, a lock if it takes a password, and whether it is connected or Windows has saved settings for it. A click on a saved one, or on one anyone may join, asks Windows to join it. A click on a network that takes a password shared by all (WPA2 or WPA3) opens a field under its row to type the password into, hidden behind dots unless the button beside it shows it; Enter or the button "Join" has Windows save settings for the network, for this user, and join it. A password is 8 to 63 ASCII characters; a key of 64 hexadecimal digits is not taken. A wrong password shows only in that the network is not joined within half a minute, after which the settings are forgotten again and the row says so. Two clicks of the right button on a saved network that is not connected have Windows forget its settings. Networks that ask for an account (WPA enterprise) or use WEP are joined in the Windows settings first; leaving a network and joining one that hides its name are told in the next bullet. The list is read every three seconds while the popup is open, and the adapters are asked to look anew every fifteen. Windows shows the networks only to applications that may use the location. The only machine Tessera was tried on has Wi-Fi switched off, so only the message that says so was seen on a running shell: the list itself, the password field, joining and forgetting a network are untested there; what is tested is how the settings handed to Windows are written.
- Leaving the Wi-Fi network and joining one that hides its name, in the same list. The connected network has a button **Disconnect**, which has every adapter leave its network (`WlanDisconnect`) and says "Disconnecting" until the network is no longer connected. Under the networks a row **Join a hidden network** unfolds a form: the name of the network, a choice of WPA2-Personal, WPA3-Personal or an open network, the password where one is needed (hidden behind dots), and **Join** (or Enter; Esc folds the form). Windows is given settings for the network that say it does not send its name (`nonBroadcast`), for this user, and asked to join; as for other networks, one not joined within half a minute has its settings forgotten again and the list says to check the password. The form says that WPA-Enterprise and WEP networks are joined in the Wi-Fi settings of Windows, which its button **Wi-Fi settings** opens. Written against the Windows API and checked to build for Windows; not run on Windows yet. Not done: a name of more than 32 bytes is refused rather than cut, and names are taken as UTF-8.

- What the machine is connected through, above that list in the same popup: each established connection with whether it reaches the internet, the IPv4 address of the machine on it and the speed its adapter states; then the VPN connections Windows keeps the settings of, each connected or disconnected with a click, with the credentials Windows has for it; then a row that opens the network settings of Windows. A VPN that a program of its own makes without telling Windows (most VPN applications) is not among them and cannot be switched here: it shows up only as a connection while it is up. Without a VPN kept by Windows, a row leads to the page of the Windows settings that adds one. A connection cannot be disabled or configured here. Under these, a switch turns Wi-Fi on and off, and one in the Bluetooth popup does the same for Bluetooth; a machine without the radio gets no switch. Tried on one machine with three connections and no VPN kept by Windows: the three were listed with their addresses and speeds, and the switches showed Wi-Fi off and Bluetooth on. Each switch was later clicked twice, once: Wi-Fi went on and off again, Bluetooth off and on again, and the switch showed each state within seconds. No VPN was connected or disconnected, and the rows that open the Windows settings were not clicked: that is untested.
- The Bluetooth devices paired with the machine, in a popup that a button in the bar opens: each with whether it is connected, and its charge for those that reported one to Windows, the connected ones first. The list is read every five seconds while the popup is open. A click on a device that Windows plays or records sound through (headphones, a speaker, a headset) asks it to connect, or to disconnect if it is connected; the row says so and is tinted until the device has done it, for twenty seconds at most. The request goes to the sound driver of the device, the way the sound settings of Windows make it, so a device without sound (a mouse, a keyboard, a controller) cannot be connected or disconnected here; a click on one says so. Pairing and removing devices are told in the next bullet. The button is there only on a machine with Bluetooth, and the icon Windows itself puts in the notification area for Bluetooth is left out of the tray strip unless the `bluetooth` option of `tray` asks for it; a machine without Bluetooth was not at hand, so the button going away was not seen. The charge is what Windows last recorded, so for a device that is not connected it may be old, and the row says so. On the one machine it was tried on, four paired devices were listed, none connected and one with a charge; a connected device and a charge that changes were not seen. Three of the four were found to be sound devices. One of them was clicked once: its row said it was connecting and stood tinted, and after twenty seconds said it was not connected again, as the device did not answer; whether it was switched on is not known. So a device that does connect, and disconnecting one, are untested.
- Pairing Bluetooth devices and removing them, in the same list. While the window of the list has the keyboard, as an open popup has, Windows is asked for devices in reach that are not paired (a `DeviceWatcher` over the selectors of classic and low energy devices that are not paired), and they are listed under **Available devices** by name, with a dot and "Searching…" while the search runs; the search stops when the window loses the keyboard, so a list on a card of the desktop, which never has it, does not search. A device without a name, one Windows says cannot be paired, and one that became paired leave the list. A click on one pairs it (`DeviceInformationCustomPairing`, on a thread of its own): a device that only wants a yes gets it, one that shows a PIN to type on it has its row say the PIN, one that shows a PIN to compare gets **Pair** and **Cancel** under its row, and one that wants a PIN typed here gets a field with **Pair** and **Cancel** (Enter and Esc do the same); Windows waits two minutes for the answer, and the row of the device being paired stays first in the list until then, also when the device is no longer found or the search stopped. When the pairing ends the paired list is read again; one that failed says why (refused, no answer in time, not in its pairing mode, cancelled, or the status Windows gave). Two clicks of the right button on a paired device remove it (`UnpairAsync`, for a device of both kinds of Bluetooth the pairing of each), the row saying in between that one more removes it. Written against the Windows API and checked to build for Windows; not run on Windows yet. Not done: devices that pair with a password or by being near (other ceremonies) are not offered the answers they need; a device of both kinds of Bluetooth may be listed twice while it is in reach; the strength of the signal is not shown.

- What went wrong, shown in the shell itself, as a shell started from the desktop has no console: a mistake in the layout, a widget nobody provides, an extension that stopped, a call to Windows that failed. A count appears in the bar while there is something on record, and a click on it lists the reports, the latest first, each with the time and how often it happened; a button forgets them all. The latest fifty are kept, in memory only, so they are gone when Tessera quits; they are also written to the standard error. Tried with a layout that has two mistakes in it: the count, the list and the button did what is said here. A layout that cannot be read at all is reported the same way, which was not tried on a running shell.
- Settings, behind the gear of a bar, in a panel in the middle of the display with the rest dimmed. Its parts: **General** (the arrangement, one of `macOS`, `Windows` and `Void`, which replaces the bars and popups of the layout and keeps the pinned applications; the language of the settings, the marketplace, the calendar, the lists of notifications and problems and the names of days and months in the clock: English, Russian or one of the [language files](CONFIGURATION.md); whether the Windows key opens the launcher; the icon packs in use, in order, moved, taken off and added from those installed), **Bars** (every bar drawn as its three parts side by side, each widget in its part under the name of what it shows; a widget is dragged to another place, part or bar, and taken out by its cross; the plus of a part opens the list of widgets to add to it, which has the widgets of Tessera first and those of each extension under its name, and leaves out the widgets made to fill a popup, which the file can still name for a bar by hand; of this the look of the parts was seen on a running shell, the dragging and the plus were not tried there; and whether the bar stays, hides by itself or hides only under windows), **Widgets** (the clock format, the first day of the week, how the processor, memory and battery readouts look, what the network widget shows, the columns of the Start grid), **Dock** (the pinned applications, with the running ones offered for pinning), **Tray** (how many icons are shown, descriptions under the pointer), **Windows** (tiling, its layout and gap, own workspaces and their keys), **Keys** (the `[[key]]` combinations: one is added, taken out, given other keys to hold and another key from a list, and made to open a popup, set the tiling, bring a window in front or move one; the program of a `run` combination is shown and written in the file by hand, the settings have no text field), **Themes** (the theme, chosen among the files of the themes directory, and the themes, icon packs and wallpapers the registries offer) and **Extensions** (the extensions and tiling layouts the registries offer). An installed extension, and a theme in use, has a **Settings** button in the marketplace that opens the settings at its card. The card of an extension also lists the shortcuts it declares, with their keys and the actions to bind other keys to. Each click rewrites `config.toml`, keeping its comments, and is applied at once while the settings stay open. An option is offered for the first widget of its name in the layout; a widget is added without options, so one that needs a popup still has to be finished in the file; popups, icon replacements, wallpapers and the settings of extensions cannot be changed there, and only the widgets of the bars are dragged. What the widgets of the bars say stays English. Tried on a running shell with the new panel: opening it, going through its parts, Russian, installing the theme `void` from a registry file on disk, choosing it, and choosing the arrangement `Void`. The choices of the Widgets, Tray and Windows parts were seen but not clicked there; they write through the same code that tests cover.
- Arranging the widgets on the bars themselves, started from **Bars** in the settings: every widget gets a breathing outline and stops answering clicks, and is dragged to another place of its bar or onto another bar, of any display, where a line shows where it would land, or off the bars by more than 40 pixels, which takes it out of the layout. A plus in each slot opens a scrolling list of the widgets there are and adds the one chosen; **Done** at the end of the bar ends it. Every change is written to the configuration file at once. What follows the pointer is drawn on the bars only, on the other bar as a pill with the name of the widget, so between the bars a widget on its way is only seen fading in its place. Tried on one machine with synthetic pointer input: a widget was moved within a slot and from one slot to another, on a bar and on the dock, one was dragged off the bar, one was added from the list, one was dragged from the bar to the dock and one from the dock to the bar of the same display. Of nine presses on a widget there, two started no drag; why was not found.
- What is playing, in the top bar: the artist and the title that the application Windows considers current reports, shown only while some application reports anything. Clicking it opens a popup with the picture the application offers for the piece, if it offers one, and buttons to skip back, pause or play, and skip on. It is read once a second, so a change shows up to a second late; the picture is read when the title or the artist changes and for a few seconds after, so one that an application swaps later under the same title stays stale. When the application tells how long the piece is, the popup also shows how far into it the player is, as two times and a wavy line; between the reports of the application the time is counted on at the usual speed, so it is off for a player that runs faster or slower. Where the application lets the position be set, a press on the line asks it to go to that place of the piece, and the line goes there at once, whether or not the application follows. Tried on a running shell: the readout and the popup, with its picture and position, for a paused player; a change of piece and a position that moves were not. Neither the buttons nor the line were pressed there, so whether an application follows them is untested.
- The output volume in the top bar. Clicking it opens a popup, which scrolls as a whole when what it holds is higher than it while each list in it keeps at least two rows and a half (not tried on a running shell), with a mute button and a slider, which also follows the mouse wheel, for the default output, the same for the default microphone, the list of outputs, where a click makes one the default, and a mute button and slider for each program that plays through the default output. Programs are told apart by the name of their file, so two of the same name share a slider, and a program whose process may not be read is left out. Making an output the default goes through an interface of Windows that is not documented, the one its own sound settings use. There is no volume for one program on an output other than the default. Changes made elsewhere show up within a second.
- The brightness of the display, as the widget `brightness-slider` for a popup, which no popup holds until `config.toml` names it, and as the widget `brightness` for a surface. It sets the display the popup is on: a monitor over DDC/CI, the built-in panel of a laptop through its driver. A display that answers neither, as many monitors do until DDC/CI is switched on in their own menu, gets a line that says so instead of the slider, and is asked again only every five seconds. The display is asked when a widget is made, as when the popup opens, and again every second while a widget shows it, every five seconds for a monitor, since DDC/CI is slow (a display that was the built-in panel is asked through its driver first, not over DDC/CI each time), so a change made with the buttons of the monitor or the keys of a laptop shows within that time.
  - Tried on one machine with three monitors: the popup on one of them showed the 100% that monitor reported, and a test set the primary monitor to the brightness it had just reported and read the same back. The slider was not dragged there, so that a monitor follows it was not seen. The path for a built-in panel has never run: there was no laptop to try it on.
- Meters in the top bar for processor load and memory in use, each a wavy line with a value, read every two seconds; memory can be shown in gigabytes and either meter as the value alone. A battery meter of the same kind, read every twenty seconds, shows nothing on a machine without a battery; the only machine Tessera was tried on has none, so that meter has never been seen working. There are no meters for network, disks, temperatures or the graphics card.
- A `command` widget for content of your own without writing an extension: it runs a program you name in the layout every few seconds, without a window, and shows the first line it prints, after an optional icon. A run is stopped after ten seconds; a program that fails, is stopped or cannot be started leaves a warning sign in the pill and the reason on the error stream. The output is read as UTF-8, so a program that prints in the console code page shows wrong characters for anything but ASCII. The default layout does not use it.
- A notification area ("system tray") in the top bar: the icons applications add with `Shell_NotifyIcon`. Pointing at an icon brings its description out of the bar under it, in a small window that the pointer goes through, unless the option `tips` is `false`; a left or right click is passed on to the application, which then opens its own window or menu. Where that opens is up to the application: one that asks Windows where its icon is (`Shell_NotifyIconGetRect`) is told the place of the icon on the bar instead of the one in Explorer's hidden taskbar, and the click itself names the point under the icon at the edge of the bar; an application that opens its menu at the pointer still does. How it works and what that costs:
  - Tessera creates an invisible window of the taskbar's class above Explorer's, which is where applications deliver their icons, and passes every message on to Explorer, so Explorer's own notification area stays complete. Edge bars, Tessera's own surfaces among them, talk to Explorer through the same window, so their requests are passed on too. At startup it broadcasts `TaskbarCreated`, the message Explorer sends after a restart, so that running applications add their icons again; an application that ignores it shows up only once it changes its icon.
  - Tried on one machine: the icons of about fifteen applications and of Windows itself (volume, microphone, Bluetooth, security, safe removal) appeared, their descriptions came out under them and followed the pointer from icon to icon, a test program that asked for the place of its icon got the rectangle the icon was drawn at, and a right click opened the menus of ShareX and of the Bluetooth icon, which belongs to Explorer; the Bluetooth menu closed again on a click on another window or on the bar. Icons that Explorer added before Tessera started and only updates since, the volume and security icons on that machine, never say which message they want and do not react to clicks. A left click was tried on one icon, that of TranslucentTB, which became the foreground application; what it then showed was not looked at.
  - The network, battery and clock items of the Windows 11 taskbar are not notification icons and do not appear. With the option `limit` at most that many icons stand in the bar, and the others wait in the overflow (see the bullet on the overflow below). The unrolling strip that `limit` gave before was tried once in a debug build with a limit of three and fourteen icons; the overflow that replaces it was not run. An application running as administrator does not receive clicks from a Tessera that does not.
  - How an application hears of its icon follows the version it chose with `NIM_SETVERSION`, as with Explorer: from version 3 on a click is followed by `NIN_SELECT` and a right click by `WM_CONTEXTMENU`, a second click soon after the first is a double click, and from version 4 on each message carries the point and the number of the icon. The pointer coming onto an icon is passed on as a move of the mouse, which some applications answer by updating their description. An application of version 4 that did not ask for the description to be shown (`NIF_SHOWTIP`) draws its own: Tessera shows none for it and tells it when the pointer comes and goes (`NIN_POPUPOPEN`, `NIN_POPUPCLOSE`), so it can open its own window there. Written against the Windows API and checked to build for Windows; not run on Windows yet. The keyboard of the strip is told in the bullet on the overflow below.
- The overflow of the notification area, which icons stand in the bar, their order, and the strip with the keyboard. An icon is named by its global identifier, or by its program and the number the program gave it, which Tessera reads on its tray thread when the icon arrives. Which icons stand in the bar follows `[tray]` (`shown`, `hidden`, entries that name an icon exactly, all icons of a program by path or file name, or one by file name and number, the closest entry deciding), then what the user chose in the settings of Windows (Taskbar > Other system tray icons, read from `HKCU\Control Panel\NotifyIconSettings` again every thirty seconds and when a new icon comes, away from the user interface, with known folders at the start of a path resolved), then `new` (`"shown"` by default, as the strip showed every icon before); `order` sets their order in the bar and the overflow alike. The `limit` of a `tray` widget still caps the bar, the icons over it going to the overflow ahead of the hidden ones. At the end of the strip, while the overflow holds icons, a button with an arrow towards the display (tip "Show hidden icons") opens the overflow: a grid of up to five icons a row, in a popup that opens beside the bar at the button, takes the keyboard and closes when another window is chosen or it has no icons left. Its icons pass clicks, the right button, the pointer coming and going and their descriptions on to the applications as the strip does, and tell the applications where they are. An icon is dragged to another place in the strip, onto the button (to the end of the overflow), into the overflow or out of it onto a strip of any display; a line marks where it goes, and the drop writes `order`, and `shown` or `hidden` where it moved, into `config.toml`, away from the user interface and keeping its comments. Shift with the right button, or the middle button, opens Tessera's own menu of an icon with **Move to overflow** or **Show in bar**; the right button alone stays the application's. On a bar that is higher than wide, the strip takes that from its own window and lays the icons in a column, two side by side where the bar is wide enough, drags go along it, and the overflow, the menus and the descriptions open beside the bar; until the bars can stand at the left and right edges, which another part of the work brings, this was not seen. The action `tray-focus`, Win+B where the keys of Windows are in use, puts the keyboard on the strip of the display the pointer is on: the arrows (and Tab, Home, End) move a ring through the icons and the button, Enter or Space chooses an icon (`NIN_KEYSELECT` from version 3 on, a click before) or opens the overflow, which the arrows then go through by rows, Shift+F10 or the menu key asks for the menu of an icon (`WM_CONTEXTMENU`), and Esc leaves, giving the keyboard back to the window that had it (from the overflow, back to its button). An application that asks for the keyboard to go back to the notification area (`NIM_SETFOCUS`), as it does after a menu opened with the keyboard closes, gets it back on the strip that chose its icon with the keyboard within the last minute; at other times the request is accepted and moves nothing, so a click with the pointer never pulls the keyboard away. Written against the Windows API and checked to build for Windows; not run on Windows yet. Not done or not known: whether Windows lets a bar that is never activated by a click (`WS_EX_NOACTIVATE`) take the keyboard after Win+B, and after `NIM_SETFOCUS` from another process, was not checked; the name of the value that holds the identifier of an icon under `NotifyIconSettings` (`IconGuid`) is not documented and was not checked, so icons with an identifier may not follow the choices of Windows; Tessera's moves are not written back to the settings of Windows; an icon dragged out of a window is drawn only inside the window it came from.

- A marketplace in the settings that lists the extensions and the themes of the configured registries and installs, updates and removes them; see [Registries](EXTENSIONS.md#registries) for what that does and does not check.
- The extension interface 0.4 ([EXTENSIONS.md](EXTENSIONS.md#interface-04)): extensions draw pictures (PNG or JPEG bytes of at most 2 MiB, an `https://` picture of a permitted host, or the icon of an application), icons of the shell's font, fields, sliders, toggles, scrolling columns and spaces; read, behind permissions listed in the manifest, what media plays and control it, the processor, memory, battery and network meters, the network connection, the windows (and bring them in front, minimize, close and move them between workspaces, as a script of KWin may), the workspaces and the colors of the theme; open and close their own popups, do the actions of Tessera their manifest names and post notices; are drawn again when what a widget watches changes, at most four times a second; answer the launcher through the runner `extensions` within 300 milliseconds, as plugins of KRunner do; have shortcuts that become actions with default keys, as the widgets of Seelen UI do; and are told when their settings change. Each extension runs on a thread of its own, so it no longer holds up the shell while it computes or asks Windows. Components built for 0.3 load and run as before, which a test checks with the `counter` example as the registry offered it; the kit gives every new export an answer that does nothing, and the example `system-monitor` uses most of the rest. The marketplace's install prompt can list the new permissions through `Permissions::wishes`. Written against the Windows API and checked to build for Windows; not run on Windows yet. Not done yet: the runner `extensions` answers once the launcher lists it among its runners; choosing an answer that calls the extension back starts `tessera do`, as the launcher can only start commands; a changed setting reaches a running extension once the settings hand it over; shortcuts of an extension installed later work from the next start; the four bundled extensions that generate their bindings themselves (`counter`, `ai-limits`, `device-battery`, `weather`) answer the four new exports by doing nothing, so `weather` and `ai-limits` use a changed setting only after they are started again.
- Icon packs ([ICON-PACKS.md](ICON-PACKS.md)): directories of `%APPDATA%\tessera\icon-packs\` with an `icon-pack.toml` that gives PNG, ICO, SVG or JPEG pictures for programs (by file name, the identifier of a packaged application or the name of a shortcut) and for kinds of files (by extension, `folder` and `drive`, and the full and empty recycle bin for the desktop). `icon_packs` in `config.toml` stacks them, the first that has an icon giving it, below the `[icons]` table; the dock, the launcher, the Start menu, the window switcher, the overview, the file manager (its folders and its files without a thumbnail) and the icons of the desktop (folders, files by extension and the Recycle Bin) ask them through the icons of Tessera. An icon marked `monochrome`, or one with a mask (named, or lying next to it as `<name>-mask.png`), is drawn in the colors of the theme and drawn again when the theme changes; SVG pictures are drawn by the renderer GPUI brings, at the size shown. The files of the packs in use are looked at every two seconds and a replaced picture shows without a restart, which the `[icons]` table still needs. - Knowing whether Tessera is the shell of the session: when the `Shell` value of Winlogon (of the user or of the machine) names `tessera.exe` and no taskbar of Explorer exists at the start, Tessera counts as the shell and leaves the taskbar of Windows alone instead of hiding it (there is none), and shows the desktop by itself. - Tessera as the shell of the session, in the place of Explorer ([SHELL.md](SHELL.md)): `tessera shell install` writes it as the `Shell` value of Winlogon of the user (no administrator needed), `tessera shell revert` removes that, and `tessera shell status` prints the values of the user, the machine, the policy and the mapping of `system.ini` with the program Windows will start, saying plainly that whether Windows reads the user's value under the `SYS:` mapping it comes with is not verified; the widget and popup `shell` and the action `shell` do the same. Started as the shell (it is then started with `--shell`), Tessera writes the start down before anything else and gives the session to Explorer in Safe Mode, while Ctrl+Shift are held, and after three starts within a minute (then also removing itself as the shell); Ctrl+Win+Alt+E starts Task Manager and hands the session to Explorer, Ctrl+Win+Alt+R starts Tessera again, both held off the user interface and ending the process if it does not quit; a Tessera that quits as the shell, fails to open its surfaces, or panics on the thread of its user interface starts Explorer as the shell once it is gone. As the shell it makes the desktop window (`Progman` with `SHELLDLL_DefView` and `WorkerW`, drawn with the wallpaper of Windows, kept below every window, answering the message that spawns `WorkerW`, and running the action `power-dialog` instead of closing on Alt+F4, or opening the popup that holds the `power` widget while no such action exists), marks it and its task manager window to Windows, opens the popup of the Windows key or the launcher on Ctrl+Esc, answers the keys of the volume and of media and "End task" (asking the window to close and ending it if it does not answer), hides minimized windows as Explorer does, signals the readiness events, and keeps four workspaces of its own by default. In both modes a thread of its own hears the shell hook of Windows as typed events for other features (with a way to answer the messages other programs send the task manager of the shell), the locking of the session, and the end of the session, at which its own thread shows the windows its workspaces hid, then lets Windows go on once the user interface has quit and given back its screen space (at most four seconds), and it takes new environment variables of the user into what it starts. Written against the Windows API and checked to build for Windows; not run on Windows yet. The notification area and the AppBar messages without Explorer, the programs started at sign-in and the dialog of `power-dialog` that Alt+F4 opens are told in their own bullets. Not done yet: the dock does not yet give the places of its buttons for the minimize animation; desktop icons are not drawn; whether Winlogon starts Tessera again after it quits or crashes (which the count of three starts relies on), and whether every Windows 11 has the undocumented `SetShellWindowEx`, `SetProgmanWindow` and `SetTaskmanWindow`, is not verified.

Written against the Windows API and checked to build for Windows; not run on Windows yet. - The notification area and the edge bars without Explorer: where no taskbar of Explorer exists, as when Tessera is the shell, the window of the taskbar's class that Tessera keeps stops passing messages on and answers them itself, deciding anew with each one, so it goes back to passing them on once Explorer's taskbar is there again. The application is told its balloon was shown when it comes as a banner, and that it timed out when that banner goes by itself or is closed; where it comes as no banner (banners off or held back by do not disturb) it is only listed and the application is told at once that it timed out, and a balloon that asks to be shown at once or not at all (`NIF_REALTIME`) is then not kept. Not done: its wishes for no sound and quiet time are not looked at beyond do not disturb, and a balloon that came as no banner is not taken out of the list when the application takes it back. Programs that reserve a screen edge with `SHAppBarMessage`, Tessera's surfaces among them, are fitted one after the other in the order they registered, a later bar at the same edge moving inward of an earlier one and one at another edge being cut short by it, on the display that holds most of each; the room each display leaves to windows is set to what the bars leave (`SPI_SETWORKAREA`, the primary display last, once the request is answered), and the other bars are told to look at their place again when one moves (`ABN_POSCHANGED`); Tessera's own surfaces, which do not receive that message, look at a number the server raises instead and take their place again within a second. Each edge of each display can have one bar that hides (`ABM_SETAUTOHIDEBAR(EX)`), which takes no room and is put above the other bars when it becomes active or moves (`ABM_ACTIVATE`, `ABM_WINDOWPOSCHANGED`); the taskbar that programs ask for (`ABM_GETTASKBARPOS`, `ABM_GETSTATE`) is the first of Tessera's surfaces that reserves an edge, on the primary display where there is one, and is said to stay above other windows. Bars of other programs are told when an application fills their display and when it stops (`ABN_FULLSCREENAPP`, looked at every second), the bars of closed windows are forgotten, and when the displays are arranged anew every bar is told to look at its place again. When Explorer's taskbar goes away while Tessera runs, `TaskbarCreated` is broadcast so that the applications bring their icons and bars to Tessera, the room Explorer's taskbar took is given back to windows, and Tessera's own surfaces register again with Tessera; when it comes back they register again with Explorer. Not done yet: asking for the taskbar to hide (`ABM_SETSTATE`) changes nothing, as Tessera's surfaces hide as the layout says; Tessera's surfaces that hide do not register as bars that hide, so `ABM_GETSTATE` never says the taskbar hides and `ABM_GETTASKBARPOS` fails while every surface hides, and programs that leave room for a taskbar that hides leave none; beside Explorer, Tessera's own surfaces still do not hear Explorer's `ABN_POSCHANGED` and keep their place when another bar moves; and beside Explorer the window is only made once a `tray` widget is shown, so in a layout without one nothing takes over the icons and bars when Explorer's taskbar goes away. Ejecting asks Windows with `CM_Request_Device_EjectW`, three times half a second apart while a program holds the device, with a buffer for the reason so that Windows shows no message of its own; a medium is ejected by locking and dismounting its volume and asking the drive to let it out. A volume that cannot be locked is never dismounted, which would cut off the files open on it: a card whose files are open is refused as in use, and only a disc opened only to be read is ejected without the lock. A notice then says that the device can be taken out, or, as an urgent notice, why not: a program is using it (with the name of the program or service where Windows gives one), the account may not eject it, or Windows refused for another reason, which it names; the row keeps saying so until the device is gone or asked again. The list is read when Windows tells the hidden window of the shell thread of a change (`WM_DEVICECHANGE`: a volume that arrived or left, or the devices that changed) and, while the list is shown, how full its drives are is read again every five seconds; reading is done away from the user interface, a reading that ends after a later one is dropped, and nothing is read while something is being ejected, so that Tessera itself does not hold the drive. Drives without a medium are looked at with the critical-error messages of Windows turned off for the reading thread, so no "There is no disk in the drive" box comes up. The action `eject` ejects the drive its `arg` names by its letter, as `tessera do eject E`. Written against the Windows API and checked to build for Windows; not run on Windows yet. Not done: AutoPlay (what to do with a drive that arrives) stays with Windows; a device that is busy is not shown with the programs that hold its files, which Windows does not tell; a notification Windows broadcasts while the shell thread is held up by a long piece of work for the Shell can be missed until the list is read the next time.
- A notice when a drive is plugged in or a disc or a card is put in, by default only while Tessera is the shell (`[notices]` with `drives = true` or `false` says otherwise): a notice with the label and the letter of the drive and the buttons **Open** and **Eject**. Notices of Tessera itself (these, the ejections above and the battery warnings below) are listed with the notifications and come as banners where `[notifications]` shows banners, for as long as a banner stays there; an urgent one comes as a banner even while banners are off or do not disturb holds them back, and stays until it is put away. A click on the banner does what its first button does, and the cross puts it away. Written against the Windows API and checked to build for Windows; not run on Windows yet. Not done: they play no sound, and with banners off (the default beside Explorer) a notice that is not urgent is only listed.
- AutoPlay for drives that arrive (this replaces the "AutoPlay stays with Windows" of the bullet above where `[files] autoplay` is on, by default as the shell, since Explorer does it beside Tessera): what the drive holds is looked at away from the user interface (its first 400 entries, at the top and one folder down), the AutoPlay events Windows names for such a drive are taken (`StorageOnArrival` for a removable drive, `CameraAlternate\ShowPicturesOnArrival` for a memory card, those of audio CDs, DVDs, Blu-ray and blank discs, and those of pictures, videos, music or mixed content), and the handlers registered for them under `AutoplayHandlers` of the user and of the machine are read with their texts. The choice made in Settings > AutoPlay (`UserChosenExecuteHandlers`, then `EventHandlersDefaultSelection`) is run at once: a handler with `InvokeProgID` through that verb of that class, one with a `CLSID` (or a `ProgID` naming one) through `IHWEventHandler::HandleEvent`, "Open folder" in Tessera Files. Where Windows is to ask, the notice of the drive gets "Choose what to do" (or a notice of its own, where drives that arrive are not told of), which opens the popup `autoplay` with the handlers, Open folder and Take no action, to choose with the pointer or the keyboard; the choice is kept for that kind of drive as Windows keeps it. `DisableAutoplay` and the policy `NoDriveTypeAutoRun` switch it off. Written against the Windows API and checked to build for Windows; not run on Windows yet; the naming of events and the choice among them are covered by unit tests. Not done: handlers that take a drop (`DropTarget`) are not offered, the icons of the handlers are not drawn, `autorun.inf` is never run (as Windows does not for drives that are no discs), and phones and cameras that come as devices without a drive letter are not looked at.
- Warnings of a battery running low, which the battery icon of Explorer gives and nothing else gives once Explorer is not the shell, by default only while Tessera is the shell (`[notices]` with `battery = true` or `false` says otherwise): at the levels the power plan in use sets for a machine on battery, a notice at the low level where the plan says to warn there, an urgent one at the reserve level that says what Windows will do at the critical level (sleep, hibernate or shut down), and one more at the critical level where the plan has Windows do nothing there, each with a button to the page **Power & battery** of the Windows settings. Each is given once on the way down; plugging the machine in starts over, and a charge that rose without it to three points above the level last warned of is warned of again when it falls, so a gauge that goes back and forth by one around a level warns once. The charge is read when Windows tells of a change of it, of the power source or of the plan (`PowerSettingRegisterNotification` with a callback, so nothing is asked over and over), and every minute where Windows does not take that request. Written against the Windows API and checked to build for Windows; tested in unit tests; not run on Windows yet. Not done: the critical action itself is left to Windows, which does it with or without Explorer.
- Applications that use the microphone, the camera or the location now, as the privacy icons of Explorer's notification area tell, as the widget `privacy`, which the built-in layouts place before the notification area: an icon for each of the three that is in use, there only while one is, whose tip names the applications, and a click opens the popup `privacy` with the list `privacy-list`: the applications under each, with a button to that page of the privacy settings of Windows. What is in use is read from the record Windows keeps of every use (`CapabilityAccessManager\ConsentStore` of the user and of the machine, where a use that goes on has no stop time), on a thread of its own that waits for that record to change and reads it then, and once a minute in case a change was not told. A packaged application is named as the Start menu names it, a program by the description in its file or else its file name. Written against the Windows API and checked to build for Windows; tested in unit tests; not run on Windows yet. Not done: a click on an application does not bring it in front, and a use by a service of the system, which Windows records for the machine, is named by its program file.
- Settings of extensions and themes in the settings, so that editing `config.toml` is optional. Extensions and themes declare what can be set in `[[setting]]` entries of one schema, `crates/tessera/src/options.rs`, shared with the settings of the built-in theme: toggles, texts, numbers, sliders, choices, colors, paths, fonts, lists and pairs, each with a label in one or several languages, a description, a default, an unset state, a condition on another setting and what it affects; there is no kind for secrets. **Extensions** shows a card for every installed extension with a control for each of its settings, the widgets each affects, Reset per setting and Reset all; one without declarations says that what it reads is written in `config.toml`. What an extension reads is the declared value checked against its declaration, or its default; a list reaches it as the text its kit splits. Every bundled extension that reads settings declares them, with the defaults and ranges of its code; tokens and keys are not declared and stay in `config.toml`. Fields write once typing pauses for 300 ms, on Enter or when they are left, sliders once they are let go, and **Browse** of a path uses the file dialog of GPUI. When only settings of extensions change, the popups, the extensions and their widgets stay as they are; a changed value reaches the running extension at once through `settings-changed` of interface 0.4, and its widgets are drawn again. The list of fonts is read from DirectWrite away from the thread that draws. Written against the Windows API and checked to build for Windows; not run on Windows yet. The search of the settings, the existing built-in rows drawn by the same controls and new built-in rows for notifications, files, clipboard, night light and startup are not done.
- Themes in layers: `theme_layers` stacks theme files over the base theme, each applied over the result so far by one rule (seed and scheme, then its tokens, then its settings, contrast once at the end; see [THEMES.md](THEMES.md)), and every layer's file is watched for changes. **Themes** in the settings adds, moves and removes layers and shows a card for each theme in use with its settings, the built-in, accent and wallpaper themes included, which declare an accent color, the scheme, corner roundness, density, font, height of the bar fields and dock size. Eleven bundled themes declare accent variants, a corner slider and more, and the themes `dense` and `round` are meant as layers. Written against the Windows API and checked to build for Windows; not run on Windows yet; how the layers are applied is checked by unit tests only.
- Bars and docks at any edge of a display, as the panels of KDE Plasma and the dock of Seelen UI stand: `edge = "left"` or `"right"` as well as `"top"` and `"bottom"`. A bar at the left or the right reserves its edge as the others do, through Explorer beside it and through Tessera's own server of edge bars where Tessera is the shell, which fits bars at the left and the right against the others and takes them off the room of windows; it slides in from its edge, and one that hides slides out to it and comes back when the pointer reaches it. Its three slots stand from the top down, the middle one in the middle where the others leave it room; each widget stands in the middle of a cell as wide as the bar, and one that is wider is cut at both sides behind a short fade into the color of the fields (on a flat bar, which has none, without a fade). While the widgets are arranged they are dragged up and down such a bar, and onto bars at other edges. Popups, menus and tips open beside a bar at the left or the right, to its right or its left, at the middle of the widget, and stay on the display; a popup that stands near the top of the display stands in the middle of the room such a bar leaves. Several bars may share one edge of a display: those that keep their edge stand one after the other from it in the order of the file, as whoever keeps the edge bars fits them, and those that hide stand one after the other beyond them, in the order of the file as well, so that one that comes in lies outside the bars that keep the edge rather than on them. A bar that keeps its edge turns solid by the maximized windows of its own display only, at the place whoever keeps the edge bars gave it. The widgets that lay themselves out down a bar today are `spacer`, `separator`, `workspaces`, which takes the direction from the shape of its own window, the dock and the notification area, which take it and the side their menus open to from the edge of their bar. These do not adapt yet and are drawn across, cut as above: `clock`, `calendar`, `media`, the `cpu`, `memory`, `disk` and `network` meters, `keyboard`, `volume`, `brightness`, `battery`, `network`, `bluetooth-button`, `label`, `command` and the widgets of extensions. A flat bar at the left or the right does not look at the desktop under it to keep its widgets readable. Written against the Windows API and checked to build for Windows; not run on Windows yet.
- Surfaces on chosen displays: `displays` takes `"all"`, `"primary"`, `"others"` (every display but the primary one), or one display or a list of them, each named by its position from the left (the first being 1), by the output GDI names (`\\.\DISPLAY2`) or by the name the monitor gives itself in its EDID, as the display settings of Windows show it, with `*` and `?` as wildcards or as a regular expression after `re:`; `[surface.per_display."<display>"]` gives a surface another `thickness`, `hide` and other widgets in `start`, `center` and `end` on the displays it names, as Seelen UI's settings by monitor do. The `displays` of a `[[card]]` chooses its displays the same way. The names are read with `QueryDisplayConfig` and `DisplayConfigGetDeviceInfo` once at start and again, away from the thread of the interface, whenever a display comes, goes or changes, after which the surfaces open, close or are refilled for the displays there are then; the surfaces of a display are found again by its output, as GPUI counts the displays anew. Widgets of a slot that an override replaces are not dragged while the widgets are arranged and are changed in the file. Written against the Windows API and checked to build for Windows; not run on Windows yet, and with one display only the selectors were not tried against real names.
- How a bar hides and looks: `show_delay` and `hide_delay` (milliseconds, 0 and 700) say how long the pointer rests at the edge before a bar that hides comes back and how long it is elsewhere before it goes. A bar that hides registers as the bar that hides at its edge of its display (`ABM_SETAUTOHIDEBAREX`), so programs that ask, as browsers shown full screen do, can leave the pointer a way to it; while one of Tessera's hides at the bottom of the primary display, Tessera's own server of edge bars says the taskbar hides (`ABM_GETSTATE`). It stays shown while the widgets are arranged, while a popup is open, and for as long as the dock asks for an application that wants attention, as its `reveal_on_attention` says. `opacity` (0.3 to 1) draws a background behind the widgets, `length` (0.2 to 1) and `align` make a bar shorter than its edge (a shorter one that keeps its edge still takes the depth of the whole edge from windows, as Windows does with every edge bar), `floating = true` or `false` gives the widgets their fields or takes them away whatever the theme's `flat` says, and `solid_when = "maximized"` turns the background opaque, with a spring, while a maximized window touches the bar on its display, which a bar looks for four times a second. What a widget reads of the theme in a handler of the pointer, rather than while it is drawn, does not see `floating`. Written against the Windows API and checked to build for Windows; not run on Windows yet.
- A menu on a click with the right button on a bar, as the panels of KDE Plasma and the toolbar of Seelen UI have: on a widget it names the widget and offers to move it to the start, the middle or the end, to remove it, to add another widget (those the settings offer, without those made for popups and cards), to arrange the widgets by hand, and the menu of the bar; on the bar where no widget is, it offers to add a widget there and to arrange them, the edge, the thickness (thin, medium and thick, or four pixels thicker or thinner), the hiding, the length and where a shorter bar stands, the displays (all, the primary, the others, or one of those connected), a new bar at an edge of the display no bar holds, removing the bar, which asks first and is not offered for the last bar, and the settings, which open at **Bars**. A menu entry that holds more replaces the menu's entries with its own, with a way back at the top. The widgets that answer the right button themselves (the dock, the notification area, the bin, the wallpaper and the workspaces) keep it; Shift with the right button, or the right button while the widgets are arranged, opens the menu of the bar over them too. Every change is written to the configuration file, which the shell then applies as it applies any change to it; a change made to the thickness or the hiding of a bar that an override for the display replaces is written to the bar, which the override still overrides on that display. Written against the Windows API and checked to build for Windows; not run on Windows yet.
- The widgets `spacer`, empty room along a bar `size` logical pixels long (12), or with `flexible = true` all the room its slot of the start or the end has left, and `separator`, a thin line across the bar in the outline color of the theme at reduced strength (the theme has no weaker outline color of its own), both drawn along bars at every edge. Checked to build for Windows; not run on Windows yet.

Motion is driven by springs: surfaces slide in from their edge at startup, dock buttons, notification icons and notifications grow in and shrink away as they come and go, the foreground indicator stretches, the wavy lines of the meters travel as their level changes, the output of a `command` fades in when it changes, popups, menus and window previews fade and slide in and out, tiled windows travel to their tiles and back, and buttons respond to hover and press. Nothing else is animated yet: text changes and the clock switch instantly, as does extension content other than its progress bars, and surfaces do not animate out on exit.

While Tessera runs:

- Both surfaces stay above other windows and reserve their screen edge, so maximized windows stop at them. While the window in front fills the whole display, as a game or a video shown full screen does, the surfaces of that display go below all windows, and come back when another window is in front; they look for that every 0.3 seconds. Tried with a borderless test window the size of one display: the bars of that display went behind it and came back when it closed, those of the other displays stayed. Not tried with a game. The window that fills the display also counts while it is not in front, as when the pointer went to another display, if it is the topmost application window of its display; this was reported for a full-screen application on a second display and changed without being tried on a running shell. A surface that sees the frame of its display change, as after another resolution was chosen, reserves its edge anew or goes to its place again; it looks for that every 0.3 seconds too. On a bar where a group at an end reaches into the middle, the middle group stands off the middle only as far as that group needs; where the three are together wider than the bar, the groups at the ends are cut where they do not fit, instead of running over the edge of the display. Widgets have no narrower form to take there. Both were written for reports from a display of 1920 by 1080 pixels and one whose resolution was changed, and neither was tried on a running shell. Windows releases that space when Tessera exits.
- The Windows taskbar is hidden. To stop it reserving space, Tessera switches it to auto-hide, which is a persistent Explorer setting. A second `tessera` process waits for the shell to exit — normally, by crashing or by being terminated — and then restores the previous auto-hide setting and shows the taskbar again. If that watchdog process is itself terminated first, the taskbar stays hidden until you turn off "Automatically hide the taskbar" in Windows Settings or restart Explorer.

Tessera injects nothing into other processes. To learn when windows open, close or come to the foreground it asks Windows for out-of-context window events (`SetWinEventHook`), which arrive in its own process; the list is also read again every five seconds for anything that goes unreported. Anything the taskbar provided that is not listed above has no replacement yet. The Windows key opens the Windows Start menu unless `config.toml` names a popup for it with `windows_key`. Then Tessera watches the keyboard with a low-level hook (`SetWindowsHookEx`), which also runs in its own process: every key is let through, and when the Windows key is released without another key having gone down, a key code that no keyboard has is pressed first, which keeps Windows from opening its menu, and the popup is opened or closed at the widget that opens it with a click. This was tried in a debug build with a window of Tessera or of a test program in front: the launcher opened with its search field ready, the Windows menu stayed closed, and a second press closed the launcher. Two presses 120 ms apart also left the Windows menu closed and the test window in front. Adding the setting to `config.toml` while Tessera ran was tried once as well: the next press opened the launcher. One combination was tried, in a release build with a test window in front: the Windows key with the up arrow maximized that window and the popup stayed closed, and the key by itself then opened and closed the popup. The setting was then taken out of the file while Tessera ran, and the key opened the Start menu of Windows again. Other combinations were not tried.

Measured on one machine (three displays, a release build, a top bar and a dock on each display with the widgets of the `void` layout, the machine otherwise in use): the shell process held about 150 MB of private memory, about 105 MB of it in the working set, and used between 0.35 and 1 second of processor time for every 10 seconds in which it was not touched, nearly all of it on the main thread; the bars were redrawn three or four times a second then. The same six bars with only a clock in each, which were not redrawn once in that time, used about 0.3 seconds for every 10, and a single bar about 0.1: GPUI wakes every window at every refresh of the display whether or not anything changed. Those figures were taken before Tessera held the wakings back: it now lets them through at every refresh only while something of its own moves or its windows get the pointer or the keys, and about twenty times a second otherwise, by putting a function of its own in the place of `RedrawWindow` in the import table of its own program. With that, six bars and six cards (twelve windows) left untouched used between 0.17 and 0.27 seconds of the main thread for every 10 in two runs, where they had used 0.39 before. A wallpaper of Tessera (`[wallpaper]`) keeps the pixels of its picture, one copy for each display, in a temporary file that Windows maps into memory and deletes when Tessera lets go of it: in one run with it the private memory was 156 MB and the working set 137 MB, about 35 MB more than without a wallpaper, which is about the size of the three pictures; that those are the pages of the file, which Windows can take back, was not measured. Opening a popup used to add about 54 MB to the private memory for two to three seconds, which took it above the 200 MB Tessera aims to stay under. GPUI has Windows choose the size a window is made with, about 2580 by 984 pixels on that machine, and what draws the window held pictures of that size (one region of 42 MB and about 11 MB more) until the window had its real size and they were let go. Tessera now has its windows made 64 pixels wide and high, by a hook on the making of windows in its own thread, and a popup, menu, tip or preview that closes is kept hidden, at most two of them, and shown again for the next one. With that, in three runs with six bars and six cards, the private memory was 155 to 159 MB at rest and 162 to 168 MB with a popup open, the first one as the later ones, and no reading in between was higher. What is left is the start: every window costs about 5 MB more for about three seconds after it is made, which was not traced further than to memory that is not of Tessera's own allocator. With six bars and no card the private memory was 133 MB at rest, 142 to 153 MB with a popup open and 173 MB at the most, in one run; with six cards more, made together with the bars, it reached 215 to 231 MB in the first seconds of six runs, above the 200 MB, and was at 155 to 159 MB three seconds later. The cards are therefore opened 3.5 seconds after the bars when the shell starts: with that it was 173 to 179 MB at the most in five runs, the highest of them with a popup opened in the second after the start, and 153 to 156 MB at rest. A menu and a preview of a window, each drawn in a window kept from a popup closed before, took it to 165 to 169 MB in one run. All of these numbers are of GPUI as published. GPUI gives every window an atlas of its own for glyphs and pictures, and its textures are 1024 by 1024 pixels at the least, 1 MB for the glyphs and 4 MB for the pictures, however little the window draws. Tessera is now built with a copy of GPUI 0.2.2 kept in `vendor/gpui`, in which these textures are 256 by 256 pixels at the least and nothing else is changed; a texture that fills up is followed by another one, as before. With it, six bars and six cards held 120 to 123 MB of private memory at rest in three runs, 130 MB with a popup open and 132 to 134 MB with the launcher open and its list scrolled, in one run each, and 160 to 163 MB at the most, at the start; six bars and no card held 104 MB at rest, 113 MB with a popup open and 161 MB at the most, in one run. The working set stayed at or below 114 MB. The start is as high without cards as with them, so the atlas is not what the first seconds cost. In one run with six bars, the memory that is there at the most and gone at rest, about 55 MB, was in three heaps of the process, private memory that is no file and no picture; in another run, with the allocator of Tessera counted, what the program itself held was never above 7 MB in the first ten seconds. So these heaps are of what Tessera calls, the system or the driver of the display adapter; which of them was not traced. Of the 155 MB at rest, what Tessera and GPUI hold through the allocator of the program was 6 MB in one run that counted it; the rest is what the graphics and text libraries of Windows and the driver hold for twelve windows, 57 MB of it before the first window is made; two bars with a clock each took 72 to 78 MB at rest and 96 MB at the most. The working set stayed at or below 121 MB in all of these. A flat bar that keeps its text readable reads the desktop under it. Windows charges the process that reads with memory for the whole desktop window, so another process of Tessera is started for each reading, which gives the pixels and ends: one such reading of a strip of 2560 by 36 pixels took 67 ms. What that process is charged with was not measured, and neither was the shell during a reading since; the 212 MB seen once during a reading before may have been the windows of the start instead, which was not told apart then. The bars used to read every five seconds, each on its own; they now have it read one at a time, when the wallpaper is seen to change and once a minute otherwise. After a wallpaper of Tessera changed from dark to bright, the fields under the widgets were there in the next picture taken, 0.9 s later; that was watched once, on one display. The motion of popups was timed once: while the calendar and the settings opened and closed, six animations of 66 to 97 frames, the view was rendered every 6 ms at the median, within 8 ms for 95 of 100 frames and after 13 ms at the longest. The bars were timed the same way: while the pointer went from widget to widget of a bar, four animations of 50 to 92 frames, a bar was rendered every 6 ms at the median, within 8 ms for 95 of 100 frames and after 18 ms at the longest; and while the widgets were arranged by hand and one was dragged along its bar and back, 802 frames in 4.9 seconds, every 6 ms at the median, within 7.4 ms for 95 of 100 and after 16 ms at the longest, three of them later than 12 ms. All of that counts renderings, not frames that reached the display. The dock was timed later, in one run: while the pointer went over its buttons, four animations of 39 to 77 frames, it was rendered every 6 ms at the median, within 8 ms for 95 of 100 frames and after 8.2 ms at the longest; the animation in which the preview of a window opened, 103 frames, had one gap of 85 ms, which is the making of the window of the preview, and was within 7.3 ms for 95 of 100 otherwise. In the same run the Bluetooth popup and the launcher opened and closed in four animations of 65 to 85 frames, every 6 ms at the median and after 8.3 ms at the longest.
